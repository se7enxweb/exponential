# Contracts and terms

This page is for designers who sell themes or theme work. It lists the topics to agree on. **It is not legal advice and
contains no contract text to copy**: have your terms written or checked by a lawyer in your jurisdiction.

## Topics to cover

| Topic | Questions to answer |
|---|---|
| Parties and scope | Who, what exactly ([What buyers receive](what-buyers-receive.md)) |
| Deliverables and acceptance | What is delivered, how it is accepted |
| Ownership | Who owns the design, client-specific assets, your reusable code |
| Licenses | GPL for the code; terms of any assets ([Licensing models](licensing-models.md)) |
| Price and payment | Model, stages, late payment |
| Changes | How change requests are estimated and approved |
| Support and maintenance | What, how long, response times ([Response times](response-times-and-commitments.md)) |
| Warranty | What you promise ([Warranty and liability](warranty-and-liability.md)) |
| Liability | Limits |
| Confidentiality and data | Client data you see ([Customer data and privacy](customer-data-and-privacy.md)) |
| Portfolio | May you show and name the work? |
| Termination | How either side ends it, what happens to access and code |
| Refunds | For products ([Refund policies](refund-policies.md)) |
| Law and venue | Which law applies |

## The GPL inside your terms

Your terms may not take away the freedoms the GPL gives buyers of GPL code. Phrase restrictions as limits of your
services ("support covers one installation"), not of the code.

## Step by step

1. Fill the table for your offer in plain words.
2. Take it to a lawyer to turn into terms.
3. Publish the terms for products; sign a contract for projects.
4. Keep signed copies and the version of the terms each sale was made under.
