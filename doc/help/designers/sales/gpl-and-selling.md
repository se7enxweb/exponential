# The GPL and selling: what is allowed

This page is for designers who think "free software" means "no money". It explains the common understanding of the
GNU General Public License in plain words. **It is not legal advice**; the license text is what counts.

## In short

- You **may** charge any price for a copy of GPL software, and for any service around it.
- Every buyer **receives the GPL's freedoms**: to run, study, change and share the code, also commercially.
- You **may not** add restrictions beyond the GPL, or withhold the source from someone you gave the program to.

The Free Software Foundation's own explanation, "Selling Free Software", is at
`https://www.gnu.org/philosophy/selling.html`.

## What this means in practice

| Situation | Under the GPL |
|---|---|
| You sell a theme for a price | Allowed |
| The buyer gives a copy to a friend | Allowed, and you cannot forbid it |
| The buyer publishes the theme on GitHub | Allowed |
| A competitor sells your GPL theme | Allowed, if they keep your notices and the license |
| You charge for support and updates delivery | Allowed; a contract about your time |
| You sell a theme as "for one site only" | Not enforceable for the GPL code; say "support covers one site" instead |

## Why people still pay

Buyers pay for what the license does not give them: your time, your expertise, the convenience of a maintained
theme, support with response times, and the trust of buying from the author.

## Common misunderstandings

- "GPL means I must give it away." No: you must give the source and the freedoms to whoever receives it, at whatever
  price you sold it.
- "I must publish my client's customizations." No: you must give the source to the people you distribute to. Work you
  do for one client and give only to them goes to that client.
- "Using a GPL theme on a website makes the site's content GPL." No: content is not code.
