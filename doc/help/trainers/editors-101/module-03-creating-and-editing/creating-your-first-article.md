# Lesson 3.2: Creating your first article

Articles are what most editors write most of the time. In this lesson learners create an article in their course
folder, meet a required rich text field, publish, and then edit the article a second time, which shows them that
every edit makes a new version.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- create an **Article** below their own folder and fill in **Title**, **Intro** and **Body**;
- explain what happens when a required field is empty at publishing;
- edit a published article and publish the change as a new version;
- choose the language before pressing **Edit** when an item has several translations.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

Source: [Creating and editing content](../../../editing-content.md) and
[The edit form](../../../editing-content.md#the-edit-form).

1. **The article's fields** (5 minutes). Create an article on the projector in your own folder. On a standard
   installation an article has **Title** (required), **Short title**, **Author**, **Intro** (rich text, required),
   **Body** (rich text), **Enable comments** and **Image**. Your training site may differ; check the form once before
   the class and adjust the exercise.
2. **Required fields** (4 minutes). Leave **Intro** empty and press **Send for publishing**. The form comes back with
   a message at the top and at the field. Fill it in and publish again.
3. **Editing** (5 minutes). On the article's page, press **Edit**. A new draft is made, a copy of the published
   version; the site keeps showing the published version until you publish. Change the title and publish: the page
   shows *Version 2*.
4. **Languages** (2 minutes). If an item exists in more than one language, choose the language in the list next to
   **Edit** first. On a single-language training site, show the list and move on.
5. **Edit conflicts** (4 minutes). If someone already has a draft of the item, the page **Possible edit conflict**
   appears first. Learners can continue one of their own drafts (select it and press **Edit selected**), start a
   **New draft** from the published version, or **Cancel**. A draft of someone else cannot be edited.

### Common questions

**What is the difference between Title and Short title?** The short title, when filled in, is used as the item's
name in lists and the tree on a standard installation; the title is the full heading.

**Can I paste from a word processor?** Yes, into the rich text fields. Lesson 4.1 shows how to check what was
pasted.

**What happens if my browser closes while I write?** The draft is saved automatically every few minutes while you
work, so a closed tab costs little. It waits under **My drafts**.

**Why do I see Possible edit conflict on my own article?** You left an earlier draft of it, for example with
**Store draft and exit**. Continue it, or make a new draft.

### Pitfalls

- **The image field.** Do not let learners upload images yet; lesson 5.3 covers it. Leave **Image** empty.
- **Two drafts by mistake.** A learner who presses **Edit** twice in two tabs gets the edit conflict page. Use it as
  a teaching moment.
- **Writing in the wrong folder.** Ask learners to check the path before **Create new**, as in lesson 3.1.

## Hands-on exercise

Use the training installation and your Editor training account.

1. Open your course folder from lesson 3.1 (below **Training**).
2. Under **Sub items**, press **Create new** and choose **Article**.
3. In **Title**, type `My first article`.
4. Leave **Intro** empty and press **Send for publishing**. Read the message at the top of the form and at the
   field.
5. In **Intro**, type two sentences about your job.
6. In **Body**, type a short paragraph about what you want to learn in this course.
7. If the form has **Author**, check that your name is filled in; add it if not.
8. Press **Send for publishing**. Note the version on the article's page.
9. Press **Edit**. Change **Title** to `My first article, edited`.
10. Press **Send for publishing** and note the version again.
11. Press **Edit** once more, then open the dashboard in a second browser tab and look at **My drafts**.
12. Back in the first tab, press **Discard draft** and confirm. Check the article's title on its page: it is the
    one from step 9.

## Expected result

- Step 4: the form refused to publish and named the empty required field.
- The article *My first article, edited* is published in the learner's folder, at *Version 2*.
- Step 11: **My drafts** listed the open draft of the article.
- After step 12 the draft is gone and the published title is unchanged.
- The trainer checks by opening one learner's article and its **Manage versions** page on the projector.

## Quiz

1. You press **Edit** on a published article. What do visitors see while you write?
2. Which two fields of a standard article are required?
   A. **Title** and **Body** B. **Title** and **Intro** C. **Intro** and **Image** D. **Author** and **Title**
3. What does the page **Possible edit conflict** let you do?
4. True or false: **Discard draft** on an existing article deletes the article.
5. An item exists in English and German. What do you do before pressing **Edit** to change the German text?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The published version; the draft is visible only to you until you publish.
2. **B.** On a standard installation; check your own installation's form.
3. Continue one of your own drafts, start a **New draft** from the published version, or **Cancel**
   ([Creating and editing content](../../../editing-content.md#when-someone-else-is-editing)).
4. **False.** It throws the draft away and keeps the published version as it was.
5. Choose German in the language list next to **Edit**.

</details>
