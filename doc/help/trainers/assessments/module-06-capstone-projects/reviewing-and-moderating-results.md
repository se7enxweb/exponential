# Lesson 6.7: Reviewing and moderating results

When several trainers assess, or the same course runs many times, results must mean the same thing each time. This
lesson closes the course with the routine that keeps them consistent: calibrate before, moderate the borderline
cases, handle appeals by the source, and use the results to improve the assessment.

> **Note**
> Results and appeals are personal data. Keep the minimum, share it only with the learner (and an employer only with
> the learner's prior agreement), and delete what you no longer need.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- calibrate scorers with sample work before a session;
- moderate borderline and must-criterion results with a second scorer;
- handle an appeal by the cited help page and the rubric's evidence;
- use question and criterion results to improve the course and the assessment.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. **Calibrate before** (3 minutes). Before a session with several scorers, each scores the same two pieces of work
   (a strong one and a borderline one) alone; compare and agree. Rewrite any level description two scorers read
   differently ([Building scoring rubrics](../module-01-designing-assessments/building-scoring-rubrics.md)).
2. **Moderate after** (4 minutes). A second scorer looks again at: every result within two points of the pass mark,
   every must criterion scored 0 or 1, and a sample of the rest. Where the two disagree, they look at the evidence
   together and record the decision and the reason.
3. **Appeals** (4 minutes). A learner who disagrees names the question or criterion. For a written answer, open the
   cited page together; for a practical task, the rubric's evidence. Correct the score if the source supports the
   learner, and fix the key or the rubric for next time.
4. **Learn from the results** (4 minutes). For each question, the share of learners who got it right; for each
   criterion, the average level. A question almost everyone misses points at the course or the question; one everyone
   gets right may be too easy. Change one thing per run
   ([Adapting and improving these courses](../../train-the-trainer/module-03-designing-a-course/adapting-and-improving-these-courses.md)).

### Common questions

**How large a sample for moderation?** Enough to see whether scorers agree: for a small group, all borderline cases
and two or three others.

**Who is the second scorer when I teach alone?** Another trainer who knows the product, scoring from the evidence you
kept; or yourself a day later, from the evidence, for the borderline cases.

### Pitfalls

- **Moderating only the fails.** Borderline passes need the same look.
- **Changing the pass mark after scoring.** Not fair. Change it for the next group, and say so.
- **Keeping everything.** Keep name, course, date, result and the moderation note; delete the rest when the purpose
  is fulfilled.

## Hands-on exercise

Moderate a set of results.

1. Take the rubric of [Lesson 4.2](../module-04-technical-courses/developers-practical-task.md) and the imagined scores
   your trainer hands out for five learners (two of them within two points of the pass mark, one with **Access
   control** at 1).
2. With a partner, decide which results go to moderation and why.
3. For the result with **Access control** at 1, write what evidence you would look at.
4. Write the moderation note for one borderline result: both scores, the evidence, the decision and the reason.
5. A learner appeals question 6 of [Lesson 4.1](../module-04-technical-courses/developers-written-test.md). Write the
   steps you take, and when you change the score.
6. From the imagined written-test results of the group, name one question to rewrite and one course change.
7. Write the record you keep for each learner after moderation.

## Expected result

- A list of results for moderation with reasons, one moderation note, an appeal procedure, one question and one
  course change chosen from the results, and a minimal record line.
- The trainer compares two pairs' moderation decisions on the projector.

## Quiz

1. Which results always get a second look?
2. What decides an appeal on a written answer?
3. Almost every learner missed one question. What are the two possible causes?
4. May you lower the pass mark after scoring because many learners failed?
5. What do you keep after moderation?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Results within two points of the pass mark, must criteria scored 0 or 1, and a sample of the rest.
2. The help page cited in the key ([Writing written tests](../module-01-designing-assessments/writing-written-tests.md)).
3. A gap in the course or an unclear question.
4. No; change it for the next group and say so.
5. Name, course, date, result and the moderation note; nothing more
   ([Giving feedback on results](../module-01-designing-assessments/giving-feedback-on-results.md)).

</details>
