# Lesson 6.1: Planning capstone projects

A capstone project is the last and largest assessment: a realistic job that needs the skills of several courses at
once, done over one to three days, and presented at the end. This lesson explains how the capstones in this module are
built and how to run one.

> **Note**
> A capstone is the trainer's own assessment, not an official certification; the partner program's certifications
> are separate and their details are not published ([Certifications](../../../partners/certifications.md)).

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- choose a capstone that matches the courses a group took;
- write a brief with a scenario, deliverables, constraints and a time box;
- set checkpoints that catch a stuck team early;
- adapt a capstone rubric and decide team or solo scoring;
- run a final presentation that is also evidence for the rubric.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. **Which capstone** (4 minutes). Pick the one whose courses the group took:

   | Capstone | Builds on |
   |---|---|
   | [6.2 An editorial site](capstone-editorial-site.md) | Editors 101 and 201, Exponential Layouts, Exponential Intelligence |
   | [6.3 Running a site](capstone-site-operations.md) | Site administration, Search, Hosting and operations |
   | [6.4 An extension and a theme](capstone-extension-and-theme.md) | Developers, Theme design |
   | [6.5 A migration project](capstone-migration-project.md) | Migrating to Exponential, Site administration or Developers |
   | [6.6 A multisite launch with a newsletter](capstone-multisite-launch.md) | Multisite and site packages, Newsletters, Search |

2. **The brief** (5 minutes). A scenario in plain words (an invented organization, never a real one, never real
   personal data), the deliverables as a numbered list, the constraints (training installation only, mail to files,
   test users at `example.invalid`), and the time box.
3. **Checkpoints** (4 minutes). At least one per half day: the team shows what exists so far. A team that has nothing
   to show at the first checkpoint gets help, not a lower score.
4. **Team or solo** (3 minutes). In teams, score the product with the rubric and each person's part with a short
   individual question at the presentation ("show me the role you made and explain one policy"). That keeps one
   strong member from carrying the team.
5. **The presentation** (4 minutes). Ten minutes per learner or team: show each deliverable on the training
   installation, then two questions from the trainer. Score while they present.

### Common questions

**One day or three?** Each capstone states a minimum. Longer gives room for quality, not for more deliverables.

**May learners use the help and each other?** Yes. A capstone is open book; it measures whether they can deliver.

**What if a team fails a checkpoint twice?** Reduce the scope with them and note it; the rubric then scores the
reduced scope, with a note in the feedback.

### Pitfalls

- **Scenarios that copy a real customer.** Invent the organization. A real name invites real data.
- **Deliverables that cannot be checked.** "A nice site" cannot; "a home page layout with a hero block and a news
  list, published" can.
- **Presentation-only scoring.** A smooth talk hides gaps; score from the installation.

## Hands-on exercise

Adapt one capstone of this module for your next group.

1. Choose the capstone that matches your group's courses.
2. Rewrite its scenario with an organization you invent, in your learners' field.
3. Check every deliverable against the courses your group took; remove those they were not taught.
4. Set the time box and two checkpoints, with what must exist at each.
5. Decide team or solo, and write the individual question for each person.
6. Write the rules: help pages open, mail to files, addresses at `example.invalid`, no live sites.
7. Write the first line of the brief: who issues it and that it is the trainer's own assessment.

## Expected result

- A one-page brief with scenario, deliverables, constraints, checkpoints, time box and the issuer line.
- A rubric adapted from the chosen capstone, with any removed deliverable also removed from it.
- The trainer reads one brief aloud and asks the group to find one deliverable that cannot be checked.

## Quiz

1. What does a capstone measure that a single practical task does not?
2. Why are checkpoints useful for the learners, not only for the trainer?
3. How do you score individuals in a team project?
4. Why invent the organization in a scenario?
5. Where does the evidence for a capstone rubric come from?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Whether the learner can combine the skills of several courses in one job.
2. A stuck team gets help in time, while there is still time to finish.
3. The product with the rubric, and each person with an individual question at the presentation.
4. A real name invites real data, and real data does not belong in class
   ([Security and privacy in class](../../train-the-trainer/module-04-delivering/security-and-privacy-in-class.md)).
5. From the training installation and the delivered documents, not from the talk alone.

</details>
