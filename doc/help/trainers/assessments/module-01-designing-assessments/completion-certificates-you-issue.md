# Lesson 1.7: Completion certificates you issue in your own name

Learners who passed an assessment often want a document that says so. A certificate of completion is fine, if it is
honest: it comes from you or your organization, it says which course and which assessment, and it claims nothing
more.

> **Note**
> The assessment behind this certificate is the trainer's own assessment, not an official certification; the partner
> program's certifications are separate and their details are not published
> ([Certifications](../../../partners/certifications.md)). A certificate from this course is issued by the trainer or
> the training organization, in their own name, and not by 7x or the Exponential Foundation.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- tell a certificate of attendance from a certificate of completion;
- write a certificate of completion with the issuer, the learner, the course, the assessment and the date;
- avoid wording and marks that suggest official status;
- keep the personal data on the certificate to the learner's name.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. **Attendance or completion** (2 minutes). Attendance means the learner was there. Completion means the learner
   passed your assessment. Say which, in the title.
2. **The parts** (4 minutes).

   | Part | Example |
   |---|---|
   | Title | Certificate of completion |
   | Issuer | `<Your name>` or `<Your organization>`, with its address or website |
   | Learner | The learner's name, nothing else |
   | Course | "Editors 101: editing with Exponential", the modules and the total hours |
   | Assessment | "Passed the trainer's written test and practical task for this course" |
   | Date and place | The date of the assessment; the city, or "online" |
   | Signature | The trainer who scored it |
   | Statement | "This certificate is issued by `<issuer>`. It is not an official certification by 7x or the Exponential Foundation." |

3. **Words to avoid** (2 minutes). "Official", "certified by 7x", "Exponential Foundation certified", "Exponential
   Certified" and logos of other organizations. The partner program uses "Exponential certified" for partner
   companies at certain levels; it is not yours to use for a learner
   ([Certifications](../../../partners/certifications.md)).
4. **The product name** (2 minutes). Use "Exponential" to name the subject, as the
   [Brand and naming rules](../../../business/brand-and-naming-rules.md) say.

### Common questions

**May I number the certificates?** Yes, and keep a list of numbers with name, course and date, so you can confirm a
certificate when someone asks. Do not publish the list.

**Can a learner who did not pass get something?** A certificate of attendance, if they attended.

**Does a certificate expire?** Yours says what was passed on which date and for which release. Name the release
(for example 6.0.15) and let readers judge.

### Pitfalls

- **Implied endorsement.** Never suggest that 7x or the Foundation checked the learner.
- **Too much personal data.** No date of birth, address or photo.
- **Scores on the certificate.** The result is "passed"; detailed scores belong in the private feedback.

## Hands-on exercise

Write the certificate of completion for a learner who passed the assessment of Editors 101.

1. Write the title and the issuer: your name or your organization.
2. Leave a field for the learner's name, shown as `<Learner name>`.
3. Write the course line with the course name and its total hours from the course overview
   ([Editors 101](../../editors-101/README.md)).
4. Write the assessment line: which parts were passed (written test, practical task).
5. Add the date, the place and the signature line.
6. Add the statement that it is not an official certification by 7x or the Exponential Foundation.
7. Check the text against the words to avoid. Remove every one you find.
8. Swap with a neighbor. Each looks for one claim the other cannot prove.

## Expected result

- A one-page certificate with every part of the table, the release taught and the statement.
- No forbidden wording, no other organization's logo, only the learner's name as personal data.
- The trainer reads one certificate aloud; the group checks the issuer and the statement.

## Quiz

1. What is the difference between a certificate of attendance and one of completion?
2. Who issues the certificate of this course?
3. Which wording is acceptable? A. "Exponential Certified Editor" B. "Certified by 7x" C. "Passed the trainer's
   assessment for the course Editors 101: editing with Exponential" D. "Official Exponential Foundation certificate"
4. What personal data belongs on the certificate?
5. Why name the release taught?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Attendance: the learner was there. Completion: the learner passed the assessment
   ([Certificates of attendance](../../train-the-trainer/module-06-assessment-and-follow-up/certificates-of-attendance.md)).
2. The trainer or the training organization, in their own name.
3. **C.** The others claim an official status that is not documented
   ([Claims you must not make](../../../advocates/claims-you-must-not-make.md)).
4. The learner's name only.
5. So that a reader knows which version the skills were shown on.

</details>
