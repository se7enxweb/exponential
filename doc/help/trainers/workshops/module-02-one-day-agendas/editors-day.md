# Agenda 2.1: The editors day

One day is enough for new editors to publish real-looking content alone, if the day focuses on the core tasks and
practises each once. This agenda is built from [Editors 101](../../editors-101/README.md); every block names the
lesson it uses.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Course | [Workshops and bootcamps](../README.md) |
| Module | [Module 2: One-day agendas](README.md) |
| Duration | 6 hours of sessions (9:00 to 16:30 with breaks and lunch) |
| Level | Beginner |
| Audience | New editors and authors, 6 to 16 learners |
| Prerequisites | Learner accounts on a shared training site with an Editor role limited to their own folder, or one installation per learner |

## Learning objectives

At the end of the day, learners can:

- sign in, find their way in the administration and open the help for the page they are on;
- create and publish an article with rich text, a link and an image with alternative text;
- edit an article, publish a second version and compare versions;
- find content with the search and bookmark it.

Cut items, in order: the comparison of versions; the bookmark; the anchor link.

## Trainer's notes

- **Keep the morning small.** Folder and article are the base for everything else; give them time.
- **Images need a prepared file.** Put two small, freely licensed images on each machine or a shared drive before the
  day.
- **Shared site?** Check the role limitation of every learner the day before
  ([Lesson 2.7 of Train the trainer](../../train-the-trainer/module-02-building-the-lab/shared-server-or-one-per-learner.md)).
- **After the day**, point learners to the rest of Editors 101 and to Editors 201.

## Hands-on exercise: the capstone of the day

Each learner, alone, in their own folder:

1. Create an article "Annual Bike Day" with an intro, a body with one heading and one link to another item of the site.
2. Add an image with a meaningful alternative text.
3. Publish it; then edit the title and publish again.
4. Find the article with the search and add it to the bookmarks.

## Expected result

- The article is published at version 2 with a heading, a link and an image with alternative text.
- It appears in the learner's bookmarks.
- The trainer checks three random learners' articles on the projector.

## Quiz (end of day)

1. Where do you see the versions of an item?
2. Why does an image need alternative text?
3. What do visitors see while you edit a published article?
4. Which button opens the help for the page you are on?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. **Manage versions** of the item ([Publishing, versions and the trash](../../../publishing-and-versions.md)).
2. Screen readers read it to people who cannot see the image; it is also shown when the image does not load.
3. The published version, until the draft is published.
4. The "?" button (**Help menu**) in the header.

</details>
