# Lesson 3.3: Timing and pacing

Most courses run late, and the part that gets cut is the practice, which is the part learners need most. This lesson
gives trainers simple rules for a timetable that holds, and a plan for the day it does not.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- plan a lesson with at least half of its time for practice;
- place breaks and buffers in a course day;
- mark in advance which parts to cut if time runs out;
- plan a day for a group with mixed speeds.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. Give four rules (5 minutes):
   - at least half of every lesson is hands-on;
   - a break at least every hour (the advocates' workshop notes say the same);
   - a 10 to 15 minute buffer after every installation step: a missing PHP extension takes time;
   - mark one "cut first" item per module before the course starts.
2. Show a mixed-group plan (5 minutes): faster learners get an extra challenge (for example the optional steps or
   the next lesson's quiz), slower ones get a helper; pairs work well.

### Common questions

**What do I cut first?** Never the exercise of a core lesson. Cut a further-reading discussion, a second example or
an optional lesson.

**How do I know a lesson's real duration?** Rehearse it with one person who has not seen it and time them.

### Pitfalls

- **Your speed is not theirs.** A trainer does an exercise three times faster than a learner.
- **Late start after lunch.** Plan the afternoon's first item as something that can start with half the room.
- **No end buffer.** Keep 15 minutes at the end of the day for questions and feedback.

## Hands-on exercise

1. Take the course you designed in lesson 3.2 (or [Module 2](../module-02-building-the-lab/README.md)).
2. For each lesson, write presenting minutes and exercise minutes. Make the exercise at least half.
3. Add a break at least every 60 minutes and a buffer after any install step.
4. Mark one "cut first" item per module.
5. Add an extra challenge for fast learners to two lessons.
6. Compare the total with the length of your course day; adjust.

## Expected result

- A timetable where every lesson is at least half practice, with hourly breaks, buffers and marked cut items.
- Two extra challenges for fast learners.
- The total fits the day with 15 minutes left at the end.

## Quiz

1. What share of a lesson should be hands-on?
2. Why add a buffer after installation steps?
3. What should you never cut when time runs short?
4. How do you find a lesson's real duration?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. At least half.
2. A missing PHP extension or a slow download takes time ([Requirements](../../../../install/02-requirements.md)).
3. The exercise of a core lesson.
4. Rehearse it with someone who has not seen it, and time them.

</details>
