# Lesson 7.4: Capstone, an editorial task end to end

The course ends with one realistic task that uses the assistant responsibly from the brief to the published page. It
is the trainer's own assessment, not an official certification; the partner program's certifications are separate and
their details are not published ([Certifications](../../../partners/certifications.md)).

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- write a safe, complete brief and get usable drafts;
- improve drafts with field actions and write alternative texts with the assistant;
- review the result with the checklist and correct it;
- send it through approval and check what it cost.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. **The task** (5 minutes). *Create a small "Winter garden tips" section in your course folder: a folder and three
   short articles for beginners, each with an image and a good alternative text, reviewed and sent for approval.
   Keep the cost under the limit set for your account.*
2. **During the exercise** (45 minutes). Walk around; answer with a question ("Which lesson covered that?"). Watch for
   personal data in briefs and unread drafts.
3. **Review** (10 minutes). Open two learners' articles and logs on the projector and go through the checklist.

### Common questions

**May I use Vector instead of the Intelligence tab?** Yes, if your role has it; plan first, then staged review.

**What if I hit my limit?** Finish by hand. Running out is part of the lesson on cost.

**Is this a certification?** No; it is the trainer's own assessment.

### Pitfalls

- **Skipping the review.** It is the point of the task.
- **Photos of people.** Use the neutral sample images.

## Hands-on exercise

Use the training installation and your course folder. Do the tasks in any order.

1. Create a folder `Winter garden tips` in your course folder.
2. Write a brief without personal data and let the assistant draft three short articles below the folder (Intelligence
   tab, or Vector with a plan).
3. Keep the drafts with **Save as drafts**.
4. Improve each draft in the edit form: one field action and one **Improve** entry per article; undo what does not fit.
5. Give each article an image and use **Write alt text**; review and correct each alternative text.
6. Review every article with the checklist of lesson 6.2 and correct it by hand.
7. Send the articles with **Send for approval** (or publish them where no approval applies).
8. Open **EI: Log** and note the total estimated cost and number of requests of your task.

## Expected result

The trainer checks each learner's work against this list:

| Check | Where to look |
|---|---|
| A brief without personal data | The conversation history or the log's turn |
| Three articles below the new folder | The folder's **Sub items** |
| Each article edited by the learner after the assistant | **Manage versions**, the label and later versions |
| Each image with a reviewed alternative text | The image items |
| No factual or link errors left | Reading the articles; checking links |
| Sent for approval or published | **My pending items** or the site |
| Cost noted and within the limit | **EI: Log** |

A learner who meets five of the seven checks has the skills of this course; the trainer suggests the lessons behind
any missing check for review.

## Quiz

1. Which three questions do you ask of a brief before sending it?
2. Why edit an AI draft before sending it, even when it looks good?
3. Where do you see the cost of your task?
4. True or false: **Send for approval** skips the approval workflow for AI drafts.
5. Name one sign in the log that a turn was expensive.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. For example: does it contain personal data; is it complete (what, where, for whom, how long); what should be left
   out.
2. To check facts, tone, links and structure; the editor is responsible for what is published.
3. In **EI: Log** (and on the dashboard page).
4. **False.** It runs the normal publish operation, so approval workflows apply.
5. Many steps or requests, a long conversation, or a strong (expensive) model.

</details>
