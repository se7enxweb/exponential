# Keeping your site healthy

This folder is for the people who already run a site on Exponential: the owner, the manager, the person who talks
to the host or the partner. It does not teach the commands; those pages exist and are linked from every page here.
It tells you what has to happen to keep the site healthy, how often, who on your team should own it, what to check,
and what to ask the people you pay.

A healthy site rests on five habits that the reliability pages for businesses also name: stay current, back up and
test the restore, keep access tight, watch the site, and know what to do on a bad day
([Reliability for businesses](../../businesses/reliability/README.md)).

## Who owns what

Name a person for each line. In a small team one person holds several; that is fine, as long as everyone knows who.

| Task | Typical owner | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Decide when to upgrade | Owner or manager | At least as often as new releases appear |
| Carry out upgrades | Host, partner or your developer | Per release |
| Backups and restore tests | Host or operator | Backups on a schedule; a restore test each quarter |
| Accounts and roles | Site administrator | When staff change; review each quarter |
| Monitoring and alerts | Host or operator | Always on; a person reads the pages weekly |
| Reporting problems | Anyone on the team, through one contact | When it happens |

## A suggested order

1. Read [Release line and support](release-line-and-support.md) and find out which version your site runs.
2. Read [Backups](backups.md) and ask your host for the date of the last [restore test](restore-test.md).
3. Go through the [Security checklist for owners](security-checklist-for-owners.md) once.
4. Fill in the [escalation contacts](../partners-and-hosts/escalation-contacts-template.md), so a bad day starts with
   a list instead of a search.
5. Keep [When the site is down](when-the-site-is-down.md) where you can find it without the site.
