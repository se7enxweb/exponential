## Two organizations, one project

| | Exponential Foundation | 7x |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | describes itself as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit corporation founded in Seattle, Washington, USA, on 19 March 2026 | describes itself as a North American web design, support, development and hosting company |
| Role | "This project is operated by the Exponential Foundation" | leads development, support and community growth since 2023; the Foundation names it the main contributor |
| Copyright | together: "Copyright (C) 1998 - 2026 7x & Exponential Foundation" | |

Exponential carried forward the code base of eZ Publish, which its creator discontinued after almost eighteen
years; the project states it is not associated with eZ Systems or Ibexa ([Who is 7x?](../../who-is-7x.md)).

## What the project commits to

From the sources quoted on the Foundation's page ([The support commitment](../../who-is-the-exponential-foundation.md#the-support-commitment)):

- Exponential 6.0 is the maintained line; security fixes are made on the main branch and published with the next
  6.0.x release; private reports get a first answer within one business day (SECURITY.md).
- Published versions never change.
- Supported PHP versions are stated in the README; the test suite runs on PHP 8.1 to 8.5.
- The documentation is free documentation in the repository.

## What the sources do not say

The Foundation's page says plainly that no public source used there answers who sits on the Foundation's board, how
it is governed, whether it has members besides its supporters, and what its budget is. A public body that needs these
facts should ask the Foundation directly. This help does not fill the gap with guesses.

## What this means for continuity

For a public body, continuity rests less on one organization than on what the license and the formats allow:

- The code is GPL and public, so any company can maintain an installation (see "Avoiding vendor lock-in").
- The partner program is open to other companies (see "Choosing a support provider").
- Your own copy of the code and your own skills keep you running whatever happens to any one organization (see
  "Composer packages and source on GitHub", "Keeping the skills in house").
