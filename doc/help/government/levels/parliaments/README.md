# Parliaments

This folder is for project managers, administrators and website creators at a parliament, a legislative assembly or
a council acting as a legislature. A parliament's website is the public record of its work: who its members are,
what is on the agenda, which bills are before it, how members voted, and what was said. Much of this comes from
parliamentary systems (bill tracking, voting systems, transcripts); the website publishes it as documents and pages.
The pages answer what parliaments publish, how to build it in Exponential, which requirements weigh most, how to run
the first 90 days, and how to publish agendas, bills and voting records.

Product facts come from the documentation of this repository; descriptions of parliaments are general public-sector
practice, and procedures differ greatly between parliaments.

## What sets parliaments apart

| Topic | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The record | agendas, bills, votes and transcripts are the official record, kept permanently |
| Sources | most of it is produced by other systems; the website must not become a second, diverging copy |
| Timing | business changes during a sitting day; votes are published within hours |
| Members | each member has a public page; their roles change with elections and appointments |
| Open data | journalists, researchers and civic technologists reuse the data |
