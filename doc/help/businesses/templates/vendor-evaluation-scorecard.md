# Vendor evaluation scorecard

This page is for owners, managers and procurement staff who must choose between two or more hosts or partners. A
weighted scorecard turns impressions into a decision you can explain. The criteria follow
[Questions to ask a partner](../choosing/questions-to-ask-a-partner.md).

> **Note**
> The worked example uses **Example assumptions** for weights and scores, with invented labels "Candidate A" and
> "Candidate B". They describe no real company. Use your own weights and score from the candidates' written answers.

## Rules for a fair comparison

- Fix criteria and weights **before** reading the proposals, and tell the candidates.
- Send every candidate the same brief and questions ([Project brief template](project-brief-template.md)).
- Score from written answers and evidence, not from a sales meeting alone.
- At least two people score independently; discuss differences above two points.
- Cost is one criterion among others; compare total cost, not the first invoice
  ([Total cost of ownership](../costs/total-cost-of-ownership.md)).

```text
Weighted total (sum of score x weight / 100)         ______         ______         ______

Knock-out criteria (any "no" excludes the candidate):
[ ] Supports a maintained Exponential 6.0.x version
[ ] Gives us administrator access and our data on request
[ ] Answers security and data protection questions in writing
```

## The scoring scale

| Score | Meaning | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | not answered or unacceptable | |
| 1 | weak, vague | |
| 2 | acceptable with gaps | written answer |
| 3 | good | written answer with detail |
| 4 | very good | written answer plus proof (a site, a document, a demonstration) |
| 5 | excellent | proof and a reference who confirms it |

For criterion 7, give the lowest five-year total a 5 and score the others down in proportion, or use bands you fix in
advance.

## A worked example of the arithmetic

Every weight and score is an **Example assumption**.

| Criterion | Weight | Candidate A score | A x weight | Candidate B score | B x weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Experience | 15 | 4 | 60 | 3 | 45 |
| 2 Understanding | 10 | 3 | 30 | 4 | 40 |
| 3 Approach | 10 | 4 | 40 | 2 | 20 |
| 4 Hosting | 10 | 3 | 30 | 4 | 40 |
| 5 Security and data | 15 | 4 | 60 | 3 | 45 |
| 6 Support | 10 | 3 | 30 | 3 | 30 |
| 7 Total cost | 15 | 3 | 45 | 5 | 75 |
| 8 Exit terms | 5 | 5 | 25 | 2 | 10 |
| 9 References | 5 | 4 | 20 | 3 | 15 |
| 10 Team | 5 | 3 | 15 | 4 | 20 |
| **Sum** | **100** | | **355** | | **340** |

Step by step: multiply each score by its weight, add the products, divide by 100.

1. Candidate A: 355 / 100 = **3.55** out of 5.
2. Candidate B: 340 / 100 = **3.40** out of 5.

Candidate A wins narrowly despite the higher cost, because of approach, security and exit terms. A difference this
small deserves a second look at the evidence before deciding.
