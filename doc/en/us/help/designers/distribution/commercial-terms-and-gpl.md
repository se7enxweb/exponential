# Commercial terms next to the GPL

This page is for designers who want to earn money with a theme. It describes options as the GPL is commonly
understood; for your contracts, ask a lawyer. Pricing is covered in [Pricing models](../sales/pricing-models.md).

## What the GPL allows you to charge for

| You may charge for | Note |
|---|---|
| A copy of the theme | The buyer may then share it under the GPL, also for free |
| Installation, customization, design work | A service, not a license |
| Support and maintenance | A contract about your time and response |
| Hosting a site with the theme | Using is not distributing |
| Early access to new versions | Once distributed, the copy is under the GPL |

## What you cannot do with GPL code

- Forbid buyers to share, change or resell the GPL code they received.
- Withhold the source of the GPL code from people you distributed it to.
- Add restrictions beyond the GPL in your terms of sale.

## Options that combine both

| Option | How it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| GPL code, separately licensed assets | Templates and code GPL; your photos or illustrations under terms you choose | The assets' license must say clearly what it covers; the theme must work without them |
| GPL code, paid services | Everything GPL; you sell support, updates delivery, customization | The most common model in GPL ecosystems |
| A proprietary theme | `LicenseRef-Proprietary` for all of it | Templates and PHP running in a GPL program may be considered a derivative work; get legal advice before choosing this |

## Step by step

1. Decide what is code (GPL) and what is an asset (your choice of terms).
2. Write the terms of sale so they cover services and assets, not freedoms the GPL grants.
3. Name the licenses in the README and on your product page, separately.
4. Have a lawyer review the terms before the first sale.
