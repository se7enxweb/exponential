# Write a plain-language summary

This page is for editors who write the first lines of a page: the **In short**, **Intro** or **Summary** field. Most
visitors read only these lines, and search results and link previews show them. Plain language guidance from
government is collected at [plainlanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/); this page gives a short method.

## Before you start

- Know who reads the page and what they want to do.
- Have the full text or document at hand.

## Steps

1. Answer three questions, one sentence each:
   - What is this?
   - Who is it for?
   - What should the reader do, or what happens next?
2. Use the words your readers use; put the official term in brackets once if it is needed.
3. Put the most important fact first (a deadline, a change, a cost).
4. Cut every sentence to one idea; aim for sentences under about 20 words.
5. Use "you" and active verbs: "You can apply until 31 March" rather than "Applications may be submitted until ...".
6. Read it aloud. If you stumble, rewrite.
7. Put it in the summary field; **Preview**; **Send for publishing**.

## Shapes that work

| Page | Shape of the summary |
|---|---|
| A service | "You can <do what> if <who>. It costs <cost>. Apply online or at <place>." |
| A report | "This report shows <main finding>. It covers <scope and period>. The main recommendations are <two or three>." |
| A notice | "<What> will <happen> at <place> from <date> to <date>. <What people should do>." |
| A consultation | "We propose <what>. Tell us what you think by <date>. Your answers help decide <what>." |

## Check the result

- [ ] Someone outside your team understands the summary without reading further.
- [ ] Dates, amounts and places are exact.
- [ ] The summary makes sense in a search result, without the rest of the page.

## Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Avoid it |
|---|---|
| Repeating the title | add information |
| Starting with the organization ("The Department of Example announces ...") | start with what matters to the reader |
| Abbreviations | write them out |
| "Please see the attached document" | the summary must stand alone |
