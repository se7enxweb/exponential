# Lesson 5.4: Accommodations and inclusive practice

Inclusion is mostly planning: asking the right question before the course, sending materials early, and building a
day that does not punish anyone for being slower, newer or different. This lesson gives trainers a short, practical
set of habits.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- ask about needs in a way that is open and respects privacy;
- plan common accommodations: materials in advance, extra time, breaks, captions, seating;
- run pair work that includes everyone;
- adapt an assessment without changing what it measures.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. Ask openly (4 minutes): one question in the pre-course survey, "Do you need any arrangements to take part?",
   with examples; no need for a reason or a diagnosis.
2. Common accommodations (6 minutes): materials a few days early (the help pages and their PDFs); extra time on
   exercises and assessments; breaks at least every hour; captions or a written summary for online sessions; a seat
   near the screen; keyboard-only routes (lesson 5.2).
3. Fair assessment (5 minutes): change the conditions (time, format), not the skill being measured.

### Common questions

**What do I do with the answers?** Use them to plan, keep them private, and delete them after the course.

**How do I pair learners?** Mix speeds, rotate roles (driver and navigator), and never make one person do all the
typing.

### Pitfalls

- **Asking in front of the group.** Ask privately.
- **Assuming.** Ask what helps; do not guess.
- **Keeping records.** Do not keep needs information longer than the course.

## Hands-on exercise

1. Write the needs question for your pre-course survey (one sentence, with three examples of arrangements).
2. For one module of this course, write three accommodations you could offer (for example: the PDFs of the lessons a
   week early; 50 percent extra time on the exercises; a keyboard-only route for the main exercise).
3. Take one quiz from this course and write an accommodated version (for example, oral answers or more time) that
   still checks the same objectives.
4. Write how and when you will delete the needs answers after the course.

## Expected result

- A needs question that asks for no reason or diagnosis.
- Three accommodations for one module.
- An accommodated quiz that checks the same objectives.
- A deletion plan for the answers.

## Quiz

1. Should learners have to give a reason for an accommodation?
2. What must an accommodated assessment keep the same?
3. Name two accommodations for an online session.
4. What do you do with needs information after the course?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. No. Ask what helps, not why.
2. What it measures (the learning objectives).
3. Captions or a written summary; materials in advance; more breaks.
4. Delete it ([Protecting people's privacy](../../../advocates/protecting-peoples-privacy.md)).

</details>
