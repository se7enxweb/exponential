# Lesson 1.6: Giving feedback on results

A score alone teaches nothing. Feedback turns the assessment into the last lesson of the course: what the learner can
already do, what is missing, and where to read next. This lesson gives a short, repeatable format.

> **Note**
> Results are personal data. Share them with the learner, and with an employer only when the learner agreed to it
> before the course. Keep them only as long as the purpose needs.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- write feedback in three parts: shown, missing, next;
- link every "missing" point to the help page that teaches it;
- deliver a "not yet" result with a clear route to a retake;
- decide who may see a result and how long it is kept.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. **Three parts** (4 minutes).
   - **Shown**: the criteria at level 2 or 3, in the words of the rubric.
   - **Missing**: the criteria at level 0 or 1, as facts ("The second version was not published"), not judgments.
   - **Next**: for each missing point, one help page to read and one thing to try on a training installation.
2. **"Not yet" instead of "failed"** (2 minutes). Say what is needed to pass and when the retake can be. The result is
   about the work, not the person.
3. **Group feedback** (2 minutes). Questions most learners got wrong point at the course, not the learners. Go through
   them with the whole group, with the cited page on the projector, and change the course next time
   ([Collecting feedback](../../train-the-trainer/module-06-assessment-and-follow-up/collecting-feedback.md)).
4. **Privacy** (2 minutes). Hand results to each learner privately. An employer gets them only if the learner agreed
   before the course. Keep the record you need (name, course, date, result) and delete the rest.

### Common questions

**Should I give the score or only the words?** Both. The score says whether the pass rule was met; the words say
what to do.

**A learner disputes an answer. What now?** Open the cited page together. If the page supports the learner, correct
the score and the key. If the page itself is wrong or unclear, report it so the help can be fixed
([Adapting and improving these courses](../../train-the-trainer/module-03-designing-a-course/adapting-and-improving-these-courses.md)).

**How long should feedback be?** Three to six lines per learner for a practical task. Longer is rarely read.

### Pitfalls

- **Only praise, or only faults.** Both parts are needed.
- **Vague next steps.** "Practice more" helps nobody; "Read the section on the trash and restore one item" does.
- **Results in a shared folder.** Send them to each learner separately.

## Hands-on exercise

Write feedback for an imagined piece of work scored with the rubric of [Lesson 1.3](building-scoring-rubrics.md):
*Article created in the right place* 3, *Required fields filled* 3, *Second version published* 1, *Item hidden* 0.

1. Write the **Shown** part from the two criteria at level 3.
2. Write the **Missing** part as two facts.
3. For each missing point, find the section of
   [Publishing, versions and the trash](../../../publishing-and-versions.md) that teaches it.
4. Write the **Next** part: the section and one thing to try.
5. Apply the pass rule of Lesson 1.3 and write the result as "passed" or "not yet", with the retake date if needed.
6. Read your feedback to a neighbor. They say whether they would know what to do next.
7. Write the one line you would keep in your records after the course.

## Expected result

- Feedback of three to six lines in the three parts, with two links to sections of the help.
- The result "not yet" (a criterion at 0) with a route to a retake.
- A record line with no more than name, course, date and result.

## Quiz

1. What are the three parts of the feedback?
2. A learner missed a criterion. What does the **Next** part contain?
3. Most of the group got one question wrong. What does that suggest?
4. Who may see a learner's result?
5. A learner shows that the cited page supports their answer. What do you do?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Shown, missing, next.
2. One help page or section to read and one thing to try on a training installation.
3. A gap in the course or an unclear question; change the course or the question
   ([Collecting feedback](../../train-the-trainer/module-06-assessment-and-follow-up/collecting-feedback.md)).
4. The learner; an employer only if the learner agreed before the course.
5. Correct the score and the answer key; report the page if it is unclear.

</details>
