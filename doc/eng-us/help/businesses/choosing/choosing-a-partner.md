# Choosing a partner

This page is for owners and managers who want a company to build, host or look after their Exponential site. It
explains which kinds of partners exist, where to look, how to compare offers and what should make you careful. It
names and ranks no company: the help never does, and the project's public directory was not open to anonymous
visitors when this was written ([Partner directories](../../partners/partner-directories.md#state-of-the-directory)).

## Do you need a partner?

| You have ... | You may not need a partner for | You probably need one for |
|---|---|---|
| A technical staff member | installation, updates, small changes | design, complex extensions, migration |
| No technical staff | nothing technical | building, hosting and support |
| A volunteer team | editing content | setup, hosting, security updates |

The documentation is free and detailed ([Getting started](../../getting-started.md), the
[installation book](../../../install/README.md)), and community channels exist (`README.md`, "Where to get more
help"). A partner adds time, experience and a contract with commitments.

## Kinds of partners

The Exponential Partner Program names these profiles ([Partner profiles](../../partners/partner-profiles.md)):

| Profile | What they do for you | Read |
|---|---|---|
| IT and development (implementation) | build sites and applications, extensions, migrations | [Implementation partners](../../partners/implementation-partners.md) |
| Creative | design, branding, themes | [Creative partners](../../partners/creative-partners.md) |
| Hosting | run the servers and often the updates | [Hosting partners](../../partners/hosting-partners.md) |
| Distribution | resell and package services | [Distribution partners](../../partners/distribution-partners.md) |

Partners have levels from Student to Platinum; higher levels have revenue, points and, from Silver, certification
requirements ([The partner program at a glance](../../partners/partner-program-at-a-glance.md)). A level tells you
about a company's commitment to the program; it is not a guarantee of quality for your project. Check the published
state of certifications on [Certifications](../../partners/certifications.md).

7x, the company that leads Exponential, also sells services directly
([Working with 7x and the project](working-with-7x-and-the-project.md)). Any other company may offer them too: the GPL
names no required supplier ([Licensing explained for buyers](../../business/licensing-for-buyers.md)).

## Where to look

- The partner program's pages on exponential.earth (<https://exponential.earth/partner>) and its listing on
  share.exponential.earth ([Partner directories](../../partners/partner-directories.md)).
- The community forums and chat named in `README.md`, "Where to get more help": ask who has done similar projects.
- 7x directly, through <https://se7enx.com/contact>.
- Agencies you already know: ask whether they have run Exponential sites, and check the answer.

## How to select, step by step

1. **Write a brief.** What you want, by when, with which budget range; use the
   [Project brief template](../templates/project-brief-template.md) and the
   [Requirements checklist](../templates/requirements-checklist.md).
2. **Ask two or three companies** for a written proposal on the same brief.
3. **Interview them** with [Questions to ask a partner](questions-to-ask-a-partner.md).
4. **Ask for evidence** (below), and call at least one reference.
5. **Score the offers** with the [Vendor evaluation scorecard](../templates/vendor-evaluation-scorecard.md), using the
   same weights for all.
6. **Compare total cost**, not the first invoice ([Total cost of ownership](../costs/total-cost-of-ownership.md)).
7. **Agree the contract**, including support, updates and exit ([Contracts and SLA basics](contracts-and-slas-basics.md)).
8. **Start small** if you can: a paid discovery phase or a small first project shows how the company works.

## Evidence to ask for

- [ ] Sites they built or run on Exponential that you may look at, with the customer's permission.
- [ ] A reference customer you may call ([Customer references](../../partners/customer-references.md) describes how
      partners handle permission).
- [ ] Who on their team will work on your project, and their experience with Exponential.
- [ ] How they keep sites current: their update procedure and how fast they apply security releases.
- [ ] How they handle personal data and what contract they offer for it
      ([Protecting customer data](../../partners/protecting-customer-data.md)).
- [ ] Whether they put custom work in extensions rather than changing kernel files, so upgrades stay cheap
      ([Budgeting for upgrades and maintenance](../costs/budgeting-for-upgrades-and-maintenance.md#keeping-the-budget-small)).
- [ ] Whether they contribute fixes back to the project ([Contributing code back](../../partners/contributing-code-back.md)).

## Warning signs

- Claims about Exponential you cannot find in its documentation, or invented customers and figures.
- A refusal to hand over the code, the database or administrator access to you, the owner.
- Custom changes inside kernel files with no plan for updates.
- No written answer on security updates, backups or response times.
- A proposal that keeps you on a version older than the maintained 6.0 line (`SECURITY.md`).
- Pressure to sign before you have seen the contract terms on exit and data.

## Keeping your freedom

The GPL lets you take your site to another company at any time. Keep that real:

- [ ] You hold administrator access and the hosting account, or can get them on request.
- [ ] The code of your site (templates, extensions) is in a repository you own or can copy.
- [ ] Backups are available to you in a documented format.
- [ ] The contract states how the handover works if either side ends it.
