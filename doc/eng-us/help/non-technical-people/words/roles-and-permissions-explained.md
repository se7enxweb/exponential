## The comparison: a key ring

In an office, nobody gets the master key on day one. A new colleague gets the keys to the front door and their own
office; the manager has a few more; only the caretaker has them all. In Exponential every permission comes from a
**role**, which is such a key ring: a set of permissions (called **policies**), each opening one tool, possibly only
in certain parts of the site or certain languages. A role does nothing until it is **assigned** to a person or a
group ([Roles for your team](../../customers/day-to-day/roles-for-your-team.md)).

## The roles you start with

A standard installation works with these roles (from the demonstration installation, as documented in
[Roles for your team](../../customers/day-to-day/roles-for-your-team.md#the-roles-you-start-with)):

| Role | Who has it | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Anonymous** | every visitor who is not signed in | reading the public parts of the site |
| **Member** | registered visitors | signing in, their own account, members-only reading |
| **Editor** | the editorial team | signing in to the administration, creating and editing content, translations, versions, two-step sign-in, help |
| **Administrator** | very few people | everything |

Your site may have extra roles, for example for a layouts editor, a person who reads form submissions or shop staff.

## Groups and sections

- **User groups** are like teams: *Editors*, *Members*, *Administrator users*. A role given to a group is given to
  everybody in it, now and later. That is why the administrator usually puts you in a group rather than giving you
  a role directly.
- **Sections** are parts of the site with their own rules, for example a members-only area or a department's pages.
  A role can be limited to one section: "may edit, but only in the *Sports* section"
  ([How do I give a group edit rights on one section?](../../manual/how-do-i-give-a-group-edit-rights-on-one-section.md)).

## How you notice your role

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Fewer tabs than a colleague | Your role does not include those tools |
| A grayed-out button | You may not use it here or now; point at it to read why ([Editing content](../../editing-content.md#move-copy-and-remove)) |
| *The requested page could not be displayed. (1)* or *Access denied* | Your role may not open this page ([When something goes wrong](../../when-something-goes-wrong.md#first-is-it-an-error)) |
| Your change becomes *pending* | Your role publishes through an approval |

None of these are errors, and none mean you did something wrong.

## Asking for access

Tell your administrator **what you want to do**, not which permission you think you need: "I need to read the answers
of the contact form" is easier to act on than "give me admin". The administrator then picks the smallest role that
does the job. If you no longer need something, say so too: fewer keys means fewer risks.

## Good habits for owners

- Give each person **their own account**. Shared accounts hide who did what.
- Keep the **Administrator** role for one or two people. Everyday editing does not need it.
- **Review the list** of people and roles a few times a year, and remove accounts of people who left
  ([Handover checklist when someone leaves](../checklists/handover-checklist-when-someone-leaves.md)).
- Use the [Team roles worksheet](../../customers/day-to-day/team-roles-worksheet.md) to write down who should have what.
