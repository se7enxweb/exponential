# Accessibility checklist in plain words

Accessibility means that people with disabilities can use your site: people who are blind and use a screen reader,
people who cannot use a mouse, people with low vision, people who are deaf, people who find complicated text hard.
Much of it is in the hands of whoever writes the content.

## What every editor does

- [ ] Every **picture** has an alternative text that says what matters in it; purely decorative pictures are the
      exception your designer handles ([Adding a picture](../first-steps/adding-a-picture.md#writing-the-alternative-text)).
- [ ] **Headings** are real headings from the paragraph styles, in order (a main heading, then sub-headings), not
      bold text.
- [ ] **Link text** says where it goes; never "click here" ([Adding a link](../first-steps/adding-a-link.md#writing-good-link-text)).
- [ ] **Plain language**: short sentences, common words, one idea per paragraph.
- [ ] **Lists** use the list buttons, not dashes typed by hand.
- [ ] **Tables** are used for data, not for layout, and have a header row.
- [ ] Information is never given **by color alone** ("the items in red are sold out").
- [ ] **Documents** are text, not scanned pictures, with headings; offer the key information on the page itself
      ([Adding a document for download](../first-steps/adding-a-document-for-download.md#good-practice)).
- [ ] **Videos** have captions, and audio has a transcript, where you publish them.

## What the owner asks for

These belong to the designer or developer, not the editor. Ask whether they are in place:

- [ ] The site can be used with the **keyboard alone**.
- [ ] Text and background have enough **contrast**.
- [ ] The site works when visitors **zoom** to 200 percent.
- [ ] Forms have **labels** and clear error messages.
- [ ] The site has been tested with a screen reader at least once
      ([Testing accessibility](../../creators/website-creators/testing-accessibility.md)).

Some sites require an alternative text for every image, and the administration's own design was made with
accessibility in mind ([Accessibility for site builders](../../creators/website-creators/accessibility-for-site-builders.md)).
Public bodies often have legal duties here; the [Government](../../government/government-start-here.md) section
explains them.

## A quick test anyone can do

1. Open a page and press the **Tab** key repeatedly. Can you see where you are, and reach every link?
2. Zoom the browser to 200 percent. Can you still read and use everything?
3. Read only the headings and link texts. Do they make sense on their own?
