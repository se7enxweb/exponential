## Why this matters

Whoever has your sign-in can change your organization's website in your name. Two simple steps make that very
unlikely: a password nobody can guess, and a second step that needs your phone or your e-mail.

## Change your password

1. Open **Change password** (in the user menu, on the dashboard or under **My account**).
2. Enter your **Current password**, then the **New password** twice. The page says what a new password needs, for
   example *At least 10 characters*, and checks it as you type.
3. Press **Change password**.

**Generate a strong password** makes one for you, and **Show** reveals what you typed
([Change your password](../../accounts-and-sign-in.md#change-your-password)).

## A good password

- **Long** beats complicated: four or five unrelated words make a strong password.
- **Unique**: never the one you use for e-mail, banking or social media.
- **Stored in a password manager**, not on a note on the screen. A password manager is an app that remembers your
  passwords for you; many are free.

The site refuses short and well-known passwords ([Keep your account safe](../../logging-into-the-editor.md#keep-your-account-safe)).

## Turn on two-step sign-in

With two-step sign-in, your password alone no longer opens your account: after it, the sign-in asks for a 6-digit
code ([Two-step sign-in](../../accounts-and-sign-in.md#two-step-sign-in)).

1. Open **My account** and press **Turn on** on the **Two-step sign-in** card.
2. Choose how you confirm it is you:
   - **Authenticator app (TOTP)**: a code from an app on your phone, such as Google Authenticator, Microsoft
     Authenticator, Authy or 1Password. Works without a network. Recommended.
   - **E-mail one-time code**: a code is sent to your e-mail address each time you sign in.
3. For the app: in the app, add an account and scan the square code (QR code) shown on the screen. Type the code the
   app shows into **Enter the code the app shows**.
4. Press **Save settings**. It applies from your next sign-in.

Each code works once, and an app's code changes every 30 seconds. Some sites require two-step sign-in for everyone and
ask you to set it up at your first sign-in.

## Lost your phone?

Ask your administrator. They can switch your account to e-mail codes or turn the second step off; you then sign in and
set up the app again on your new phone ([How a user who lost their phone signs in](../../manual/a-user-lost-their-phone-how-do-they-sign-in.md)).

## Five habits

1. Use **your own account**, never a shared one.
2. **Never send your password** by e-mail or chat, not even to an administrator; nobody needs it.
3. **Sign out** on shared computers.
4. Be suspicious of e-mails asking you to "confirm your password" through a link. Open the administration from your
   own bookmark instead.
5. Tell your administrator at once if you think someone else knows your password.
