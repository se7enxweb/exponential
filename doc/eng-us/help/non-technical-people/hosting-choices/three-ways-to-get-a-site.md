## Why not do it yourself?

You could, and nothing stops you from learning. But installing, updating and backing up Exponential means working on a
server with a command line ([What you never have to do](../what-you-never-have-to-do.md)). For most non-technical
people, an hour of someone else's skilled time is cheaper than a weekend of their own, and safer. If you want to try
it anyway, the [Individuals](../../individuals/individuals-start-here.md) section shows what running a small site alone
involves.

## Way 1: managed hosting

A company runs the server **and** Exponential for you: installation, updates, backups and HTTPS are part of the
service ([Managed hosting](../../hosting/models/managed-hosting.md)). You sign in and edit.

- 7x, the company that maintains Exponential, sells managed hosting ("Exponential Website Hosting") and support
  contracts; prices and terms are on its website, not in this help ([Who is 7x?](../../who-is-7x.md#services-and-hosting)).
- Hosting partners of the project may offer the same ([Hosting partners](../../partners/hosting-partners.md)).

**Good for:** people who want to write, not to manage anything. **Watch:** what exactly is included, and how you leave.

## Way 2: a host and a developer

You rent hosting from a general hosting company, and a developer or freelancer installs and looks after Exponential on
it. Ordinary shared hosting can work if it meets the requirements; Exponential can run with SQLite, which keeps the
setup simple ([Shared hosting](../../hosting/models/shared-hosting.md),
[Requirements at a glance](../../hosting/basics/requirements-at-a-glance.md)).

**Good for:** small budgets with a trusted developer nearby. **Watch:** two parties, so agree clearly who does
updates and backups, and what happens when the developer is unavailable
([Who does what](../../customers/partners-and-hosts/who-does-what.md)).

## Way 3: an Exponential Partner

An Exponential Partner is a company in the project's Partner Program: it builds, hosts or supports Exponential sites
and works directly with 7x and the Exponential Foundation ([Partners: start here](../../partners/partners-start-here.md)).
A partner can do everything: plan and build the site, host it, train your team and look after it.

**Good for:** organizations that want one responsible party and a site built for them. **Watch:** the project scope
and the yearly maintenance, in writing ([Choosing a partner](../../customers/partners-and-hosts/choosing-a-partner.md)).

## Side by side

| | Managed hosting | Host and developer | Partner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who you call | One company | Host for the server, developer for the site | One company |
| Your technical work | None | None, if agreed well | None |
| Design and building | Usually a shipped design, or extra | The developer | Included in the project |
| Typical cost shape | Monthly fee | Monthly hosting plus hourly work | Project price plus maintenance agreement |
| Main risk | Unclear exit terms | Gaps between the two parties | Scope creep |

This help names no prices; ask for offers and compare them with [Questions about cost](questions-about-cost.md).

## How to choose

1. Write down what the site must do, in a few lines.
2. Decide how much time per month you or your team will spend on content. Less time means more service.
3. Ask two or three providers the [questions before you sign](what-to-ask-before-you-sign.md).
4. Prefer the one whose answers you understand. Clear answers to a non-technical person are a good sign.
