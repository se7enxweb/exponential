# Response times and commitments

This page is for designers who write promises into support offers. A missed promise costs more trust than a modest
promise kept.

## Terms to define

| Term | Define |
|---|---|
| First response | When the customer hears from a person that the request is being handled |
| Resolution | When the problem is fixed, worked around or answered |
| Working hours | Days, hours and time zone, holidays |
| Severity | For example: site down; a feature broken; a cosmetic issue; a question |
| Clock stops | While you wait for the customer's information or access |

## Severity levels, as an example structure

| Level | Meaning | First response | Resolution target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The site is down because of the theme | (your value) | (your value) |
| 2 | A main feature broken | (your value) | (your value) |
| 3 | A minor or visual issue | (your value) | Next release |
| 4 | A question | (your value) | - |

## Step by step

1. Look at your actual availability over a normal month.
2. Set values below that, with room for illness and holidays.
3. Arrange a backup person for level 1, or do not promise level 1 outside working hours.
4. State what you do when you miss a time (a credit, an apology, an escalation) in the terms.
5. Measure: write down when each request arrived, was answered and was resolved.
