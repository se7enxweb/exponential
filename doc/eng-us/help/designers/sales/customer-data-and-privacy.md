# Customer data and privacy

This page is for designers who sell and support themes. You will meet personal data: your buyers' names and addresses,
and the content and users of your clients' sites. Data protection law applies in many countries; ask a lawyer what
applies to you. **This is not legal advice.**

## Where personal data appears

| Where | Rule |
|---|---|
| Orders and invoices | Keep only what you need; store it safely; publish a privacy notice |
| Access to a client's site | Use an account of your own with the least rights; remove it when done |
| Database copies for debugging | Avoid them; if needed, anonymize first and delete afterwards |
| Screenshots | Only demo content ([Screenshot checklist](../distribution/screenshot-checklist.md)) |
| Bug reports and public issues | Never paste names, emails, addresses, passwords or session data |
| Demo content and seed data | Invented, never copied from a client ([Demo content](../distribution/demo-content.md)) |
| Case studies | Name a client only with written consent ([Case studies](../../business/case-studies-and-content-marketing.md)) |

## Step by step

1. List where personal data reaches you.
2. For each place, decide: avoid it, minimize it, or protect it.
3. Write a privacy notice for your site and a short data clause for your contracts.
4. Clean up: remove old accounts, database copies and exports regularly.

## When something leaks

If personal data was published by mistake (a screenshot, a commit), remove it, tell the client, and follow the
reporting duties of your law. A pushed commit stays in the history: ask for help before rewriting it.
