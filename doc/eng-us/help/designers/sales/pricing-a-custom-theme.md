# Pricing a custom theme project

This page is for designers who answer a client's "how much for a design for our site?". A good quote is mostly a good
description of the work.

## The parts of a quote

| Part | Contents |
|---|---|
| Scope | Pages and view modes to design: front page, article, list, search, forms, error pages |
| Phases | Discovery, design drafts, build, review, launch, handover |
| Assumptions | Content classes exist; content is delivered by the client; one language; one siteaccess |
| Quality | Accessibility level, browsers and widths tested, dark mode yes or no |
| Excluded | Content entry, hosting, extensions beyond the theme |
| Changes | How a change request is estimated and approved |
| License | Who owns what; GPL code; client-specific assets ([Contracts and terms](contracts-and-terms.md)) |
| Price and payment | The model ([Pricing models](pricing-models.md)) and payment stages |

## Step by step

1. Hold a discovery conversation; write down what the site must do and for whom.
2. List the templates and view modes needed (the planning pages of the leadership help help here:
   [Estimating and scoping with extensions](../../leadership/estimating-and-scoping-with-extensions.md)).
3. Estimate each item ([Estimating theme work](estimating-theme-work.md)).
4. Write the quote with the parts above.
5. Ask the client to confirm the assumptions in writing.

## Good practice

- Price the review rounds explicitly; they are where projects grow.
- Include the handover: documentation and a session for editors.
- Offer maintenance separately ([Maintenance subscriptions](maintenance-subscriptions.md)).
