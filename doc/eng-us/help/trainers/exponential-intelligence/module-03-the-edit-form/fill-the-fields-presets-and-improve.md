# Lesson 3.2: Fill the fields, presets and the Improve menu

Three more helpers live in the edit form: **Fill the fields** writes a whole new item from a brief, **presets** do
routine jobs (a title, a summary, metadata) with one click, and the **Improve** menu polishes a text. All of them put
values into the form for review; none stores or publishes anything.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- open the edit form's **Exponential Intelligence** area and use **Fill the fields** with a brief;
- use the presets **Suggest title**, **Write summary**, **Meta description** and **Keywords** where a class has them;
- use the **Improve** menu: **Shorten**, **Expand**, **Clarify**, **Fix grammar**, **Simplify for new readers**,
  **More formal**, **More casual**;
- undo all filled values at once.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

Source: the edit form and presets pages under **EI: Help** (`extension/expai/doc/edit-view-integration.md`,
`extension/expai/doc/presets.md`).

1. **The area** (3 minutes). Above the fields, the **Exponential Intelligence** area is closed until **Show**; the
   choice is remembered. Its first section is **Fill the fields**.
2. **Fill the fields** (4 minutes). Type a brief; the model gets the class's writable fields (with format hints,
   required flags and maximum lengths) and answers with values that are put into the form. **Undo** restores all of
   them. Images, relations and files are not filled.
3. **Presets** (3 minutes). One button per preset next to the fields a class maps it to: on a standard article,
   **Suggest title** on the title and **Write summary** on the intro; classes with a metadata field also get **Meta
   description** and **Keywords**. A class without a mapping gets no presets.
4. **Improve** (3 minutes). On every text field, the **Improve** menu offers seven rewrites. Each works like a field
   action: the answer goes into the field with a note, **Undo** and **View record**.
5. **Keep the human voice** (2 minutes). Use presets for routine work; write the parts that need your knowledge
   yourself.

### Common questions

**Is Fill the fields the same as asking in the Intelligence tab?** Similar result, different place: the tab creates
drafts below an item; Fill the fields fills the form you have open.

**Why do I see no presets?** The class has no preset mapping on your installation.

**Is the meta description important?** It is what search engines often show; a preset is a good start, but read it.

### Pitfalls

- **Filling over existing work.** **Fill the fields** replaces values; use it on a new item, or **Undo**.
- **Generic metadata.** Keywords suggested for every page look alike; adjust them.
- **Expand on short texts.** It invents detail; check facts.

## Hands-on exercise

Use the training installation and your course folder.

1. In your course folder, press **Create new** and choose **Article**.
2. Open the **Exponential Intelligence** area with **Show**.
3. In **Fill the fields**, type a brief, for example `A short article for beginners on watering houseplants in winter,
   about 150 words, friendly tone, no brand names.` Send it.
4. Read every filled field. Press **Undo** once to see all values go, then fill again.
5. On the title, use **Suggest title** (if offered) and choose a title.
6. On the intro, use **Write summary** (if offered).
7. On the body, open **Improve** and choose **Fix grammar**, then **Simplify for new readers**. Undo the one you do not
   like.
8. Publish the article with **Send for publishing**, or store it as a draft.

## Expected result

- A new article filled from the brief, with a title and summary from presets where offered, polished with **Improve**,
  stored or published by the learner.
- The trainer checks by opening one learner's article and asking which parts they changed by hand.

## Quiz

1. What does **Fill the fields** do?
2. Name three entries of the **Improve** menu.
3. Why might an article form show no presets?
4. True or false: presets publish the item.
5. Which fields does **Fill the fields** not fill?
   A. Text fields B. Rich text fields C. Images, relations and files D. The title

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. It writes values for the form's writable fields from a brief and puts them into the form.
2. Any three of **Shorten**, **Expand**, **Clarify**, **Fix grammar**, **Simplify for new readers**, **More formal**,
   **More casual**.
3. The class has no preset mapping on that installation.
4. **False.** They put values into the form; nothing is stored until you store the draft.
5. **C.**

</details>
