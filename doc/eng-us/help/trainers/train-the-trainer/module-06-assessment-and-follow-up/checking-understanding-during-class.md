# Lesson 6.1: Checking understanding during class

The worst time to discover that half the room did not follow is the final test. Every lesson in these courses has an
**Expected result** for that reason: it is a check the trainer can do while there is still time to help. This lesson
shows four quick checks and when to use them.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- use a lesson's **Expected result** as a check on every learner's screen;
- ask "show me" questions instead of "any questions?";
- run a quick poll with one question per objective;
- use a one-minute written summary at the end of a module.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. Four checks (8 minutes):
   - **Expected result**: walk the room (or ask for a screenshot of the window only, online) and compare with the
     lesson's expected result.
   - **Show me**: "Show me where you would change your password" is better than "Is that clear?".
   - **Poll**: one multiple-choice question per objective; the quiz questions of the lesson serve.
   - **One-minute summary**: at the end of a module, each learner writes the most important thing and the least clear
     thing.
2. Act on it (2 minutes): if more than a few learners miss a point, re-teach it briefly before going on.

### Common questions

**Do I grade these?** No. They are for steering the course.

**What if one learner is far behind?** A helper or a pair partner; keep the room moving.

### Pitfalls

- **"Any questions?"** Silence does not mean understanding.
- **Checking only the fast learners.** Look at every screen.

## Hands-on exercise

1. Pick one lesson of this course.
2. Write two "show me" questions for it, each tied to one objective.
3. Turn two of its quiz questions into a quick poll.
4. Write the one-minute summary prompt for the end of its module.
5. Try them on a partner and note which revealed a misunderstanding.

## Expected result

- Two "show me" questions, a two-question poll and a summary prompt for one lesson.
- At least one check revealed something the partner was unsure about.

## Quiz

1. Why is "Any questions?" a weak check?
2. Which section of every lesson is a ready-made check?
3. What do learners write in a one-minute summary?
4. Are these checks graded?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Silence does not mean understanding.
2. **Expected result** (and the quiz).
3. The most important thing and the least clear thing.
4. No; they steer the course.

</details>
