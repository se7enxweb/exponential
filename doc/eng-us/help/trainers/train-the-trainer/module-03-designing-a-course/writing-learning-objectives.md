# Lesson 3.1: Writing learning objectives

Every lesson in these courses starts with "After this lesson, learners can:" and a short list. Those lines are the
most important part of a lesson: they decide what you show, what learners practise and what the quiz asks. This
lesson teaches trainers to write such objectives for their own lessons.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- tell an observable objective ("publish an article") from an unobservable one ("understand publishing");
- write three to five objectives for a lesson, each starting with an action verb;
- link each objective to one step of the exercise and one quiz question;
- cut content that serves no objective.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. Show two versions on the board (5 minutes):
   - weak: "Learners understand versions."
   - strong: "Learners can open **Manage versions** for an item and say which version is published."
2. Give the rule (5 minutes): an objective names something the learner *does* that the trainer can *see*: open,
   create, publish, assign, find, explain in one sentence, choose between two options.
3. Show the alignment table of any lesson in this course (5 minutes): each objective has an exercise step and a quiz
   question. If one is missing, the lesson has a gap.

### Common questions

**How many objectives per lesson?** Three to five. More means the lesson is too big; split it.

**Are "know" and "understand" forbidden?** Not forbidden, but not checkable. Replace them by what knowing looks like:
"explain in one sentence", "choose the right ... for ...".

### Pitfalls

- **Objectives about the trainer.** "Show the role editor" is the trainer's plan, not a learner outcome.
- **Objectives that need another installation.** If the lab cannot do it, the learner cannot either.
- **Too abstract.** Tie objectives to real screens and commands of Exponential.

## Hands-on exercise

Write objectives for a 45-minute lesson on bookmarks for editors.

1. Read the guide [Bookmarks](../../../../guides/bookmarks.md) in the Help center.
2. Write five candidate objectives. Start each with "After this lesson, learners can".
3. Mark every verb. Replace any of "know", "understand", "learn", "be aware of" by an observable verb.
4. Keep the best three to five.
5. For each objective, write the exercise step that practises it (with the real button names from the guide).
6. For each objective, write one quiz question that checks it.
7. Exchange with a partner. The partner marks any objective they could not check in class.

## Expected result

- Three to five objectives with observable verbs, each with one exercise step and one quiz question.
- The partner could check every objective in class.
- The trainer collects one example per learner and discusses one strong and one weak one.

## Quiz

1. Rewrite as an observable objective: "Learners understand roles."
2. How many objectives should a 45-minute lesson have?
3. What do you do with content that serves no objective?
4. Why should every objective have an exercise step?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. For example: "Learners can open a role in **Users > Roles and policies** and say which groups it is assigned to."
2. Three to five.
3. Cut it, or move it to further reading.
4. So learners practise it and the trainer can see whether they can do it.

</details>
