# Lesson 1.3: Knowing your learners

The same software looks very different to an editor who publishes news every morning, an administrator who keeps the
site safe and a developer who writes templates. The help of Exponential is organized by exactly these groups, its
**audiences**, and the trainer courses follow them. This lesson shows how to find out who your learners are before
the course and how to choose the right course, modules and pace for them.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- name the audiences the Help center uses and the course that fits each one;
- write a five-question pre-course survey that collects no personal data beyond what the course needs;
- choose between a full course, selected modules and a workshop for a given group;
- recognize a mixed group and plan for it.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. Open the Help center dashboard and read the audience cards under **Start where you are** aloud: Editors,
   Administrators, Developers, Users, Project managers, Sales & marketing, Partners, Advocates, Designers, Hosting
   companies, Application creators, Website creators, Supporters, Sponsors (3 minutes).
2. Map them to the courses (4 minutes):

   | Learners | Course to start with |
   |---|---|
   | People who write and publish content | Editors 101, then Editors 201 |
   | People who run a site: users, roles, caches, security, updates | Site administration |
   | People who build on Exponential: templates, classes, extensions | Developers |
   | People who design the look of a site or a theme | Theme design |
   | People who build pages with blocks | Exponential Layouts |
   | People who run servers for Exponential sites | Hosting and operations |
   | Teams moving a site from another system | Migration |

3. Show a sample survey (3 minutes) and point out what is *not* asked: no date of birth, no private address.

### Common questions

**What if half the group are editors and half are developers?** Teach the shared part together (the Editors 101
modules on the content tree and publishing are useful to everyone), then split, or run the developer part on another
day. Lesson 3.3 has timing plans for this.

**Should managers attend?** A project manager usually gets more from a short overview and the Project managers pages
of the help than from a hands-on lab.

### Pitfalls

- **Trusting job titles.** "Webmaster" can mean editor, administrator or developer. Ask what people do each week.
- **Collecting too much.** A survey only needs what changes your plan. Store answers no longer than the course needs.
- **Forgetting accessibility needs.** Ask openly whether anyone needs materials in another format or other
  arrangements (Module 5).

## Hands-on exercise

Prepare the intake for a real or imagined group of eight learners.

1. Open the Help center and note the audience labels exactly as the dashboard shows them.
2. Write a pre-course survey with these five questions (adapt the wording):
   1. What do you do with the website each week? (publish content / manage users and settings / write code or
      templates / design / run servers / other)
   2. Which version of Exponential do you use, if you know? (the version is shown on the administration dashboard
      under **System**)
   3. Have you used another content management system? Which?
   4. What must you be able to do after the course?
   5. Do you need any arrangements to take part (format of materials, breaks, captions)?
3. Answer it yourself for three invented learners (for example an editor, an administrator, a front-end developer).
4. For each, choose the course from the table in the trainer's notes and the first two modules.
5. Decide whether this group gets one shared course, two parallel tracks or a shared morning and split afternoon.
6. Write one sentence for the course invitation that says who the course is for.

## Expected result

- A five-question survey that asks nothing that the course does not need.
- Three learner profiles, each with a course and the first two modules named.
- A decision (shared, parallel or split) with one sentence of reasoning, and an invitation sentence.
- The trainer reviews: does any question collect personal data that is not needed? Is every course name real?

## Quiz

1. Name four audiences that the Help center dashboard shows.
2. Which course fits a learner who manages users, roles and caches?
3. Why should a survey not ask for a learner's date of birth?
4. A group has editors and developers. Name one way to plan for it.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Any four of: Editors, Administrators, Developers, Users, Project managers, Sales & marketing, Partners,
   Advocates, Designers, Hosting companies, Application creators, Website creators, Supporters, Sponsors.
2. Site administration.
3. It does not change the course plan; collect only what the course needs
   ([Protecting people's privacy](../../../advocates/protecting-peoples-privacy.md)).
4. A shared morning on content and publishing and split afternoons, or two parallel tracks, or two days.

</details>
