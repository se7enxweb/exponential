# Lesson 1.4: Your course material and the GNU FDL

Trainers copy, print and change documentation all the time: a handout, a slide with a screenshot, a translated
exercise. The Exponential documentation, these trainer courses included, is free documentation under the GNU Free
Documentation License (GFDL) 1.3 or any later version. This lesson explains in practical terms what that allows and
what it asks of you, so your course folder is correct from the start.

> **Note**
> This lesson is a summary, not legal advice. The license text in `doc/en/us/LICENSE-GFDL-1.3.txt` is what counts.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- say what the GFDL allows: read, copy, share, translate, adapt and combine the pages;
- list what must travel with a copy: the copyright notices, the license notice and a copy of the license;
- mark an adapted lesson correctly (same license, a note of the change, a title of its own);
- keep their own independent slides separate from licensed pages in a course folder.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. Open [Free documentation](../../../free-documentation.md) and go through its headings: Read and study it; Copy it
   and share it; Translate it; Adapt and improve it; Combine it with other works; Cite it (8 minutes).
2. Show the end of any help page: the line that says the page is free to copy, translate and improve under the GNU
   FDL 1.3. Every help page, its print view and its PDF end with the license notice (2 minutes).
3. Discuss two cases (5 minutes): a printed handout of three lessons for twelve learners (allowed; keep the notices
   and give the license with it), and a reworked lesson for your company intranet (allowed; publish it under the same
   license, say it was changed and by whom, and give it its own title).

### Common questions

**May I charge for my course?** The license lets you give copies "for free or for a fee". Your teaching time is
yours to charge for.

**Do my own slides become GFDL?** No. Independent works placed next to the pages in a collection keep their own terms.
Pages from this documentation, and versions made from them, carry the GFDL.

**What about more than 100 printed copies?** The license then asks for a cover with the full title and you as the
publisher, and for copies in a hard-to-edit format, an editable copy or where to get one. Section 3 of the license
has the details.

**Some old pages have other terms.** Pages inherited from earlier versions keep their own notice. Check
[The license of this documentation](../../../../README-LICENSE.md) before you copy them.

### Pitfalls

- **Removing the footer.** A handout without the license notice breaks the conditions.
- **Keeping the original title on a changed version.** Give your version its own title unless the publisher of the
  original agrees.
- **Screenshots with personal data.** The license is about the text; privacy is about people. Never put real
  user names or e-mail addresses on a slide.

## Hands-on exercise

Build the license part of a course folder for a two-hour editor session.

1. Choose two help pages for the handout, for example [Creating and editing content](../../../editing-content.md)
   and [Publishing, versions and the trash](../../../publishing-and-versions.md).
2. In the Help center, open each page and press **Download as PDF**. Check that the last part of each PDF is the
   license notice.
3. Copy `doc/en/us/LICENSE-GFDL-1.3.txt` from the installation (or download the license from the GNU website) into the
   course folder.
4. Take a third page, [Getting started](../../../getting-started.md), and adapt it: shorten it to the steps your
   learners need. Give it a new title, for example "Getting started at our office".
5. At the top or end of the adapted file, add: the original title and where it comes from, a note that you changed
   it, your name or organization and the date, and the license notice (GNU FDL 1.3 or any later version).
6. Put your own slides in a separate subfolder and note their own license or copyright.
7. Write a `README.txt` for the folder that lists each file and its license.

## Expected result

- The folder holds two unchanged PDFs with their license notices, the license text, one adapted page marked as
  changed with a title of its own, your own slides in a separate subfolder, and a `README.txt` naming the license of
  each file.
- The trainer checks the adapted page: same license, change noted, new title, original copyright kept.

## Quiz

1. Which three things must travel with a copy of a help page?
2. True or false: you may sell printed copies of the help pages.
3. You rewrote a lesson for your team. Under which license must you share your version?
4. Do your own independent slides fall under the GFDL because they sit in the same folder as GFDL pages?
5. Where is the license text in an installation?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The copyright notices, the license notice and a copy of the license.
2. **True.** Copies may be given for free or for a fee, on the license's conditions.
3. The same license, the GNU Free Documentation License (1.3 or any later version).
4. **No.** Independent works combined in a collection keep their own terms.
5. `doc/en/us/LICENSE-GFDL-1.3.txt` ([Free documentation](../../../free-documentation.md)).

</details>
