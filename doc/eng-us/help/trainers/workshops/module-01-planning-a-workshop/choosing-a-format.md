# Lesson 1.1: Choosing a workshop format

Full courses are not always possible. Many groups have one day, or one intensive week. This course offers ready
agendas for those cases, built from the lessons of the subject courses. This lesson helps trainers choose the right
format before they choose the content.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- name the four formats of this course and what each can achieve;
- choose a format from the group's goal, available time and starting level;
- pick the matching agenda page;
- explain to a client what a one-day workshop cannot do.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. Show the formats (5 minutes):

   | Format | Achieves | Agendas |
   |---|---|---|
   | Half day | A first look; a decision whether to go further | [Decision makers' half day](../module-02-one-day-agendas/decision-makers-half-day.md) |
   | One day | One role's core tasks, practised once | [Module 2](../module-02-one-day-agendas/README.md) |
   | Three days | One role's tasks practised until learners can work alone | [Module 3](../module-03-three-day-bootcamps/README.md) |
   | Five days | Several roles, or one role in depth, with a capstone project | [Module 4](../module-04-five-day-bootcamps/README.md) |

2. Explain the trade-off (5 minutes): a one-day workshop shows and practises; it does not make an administrator.
   Follow-up material (the subject courses) matters most after short formats.

### Common questions

**Can a one-day workshop replace a course?** For a small editorial team with simple needs, sometimes. For
administrators and developers, no.

**Can we split five days over five weeks?** Yes, and retention is often better; keep the capstone project going
between sessions.

### Pitfalls

- **Too much in one day.** Pick fewer topics and practise them.
- **Mixed roles in a bootcamp.** Use the mixed-group plans or split the group.

## Hands-on exercise

For each of these invented groups, choose a format and an agenda page, and write one sentence why.

1. A city library: five editors who will publish news, one day available.
2. A web agency: four PHP developers starting their first Exponential project next month, one week available.
3. A university IT department: two administrators taking over an existing site, three days available.
4. A board of a nonprofit deciding whether to adopt Exponential: one afternoon.
5. A hosting company's support team of six: two days available.

## Expected result

- 1: one-day editors agenda; 2: five-day full-stack developer week; 3: three-day administrator bootcamp; 4:
  decision makers' half day; 5: the one-day administrators agenda plus a day of the Hosting and operations course.
- Each choice has one sentence of reasoning that names the goal and the time.

## Quiz

1. Which format suits a group that must work alone afterwards?
2. What can a one-day workshop not achieve for administrators?
3. Why is follow-up material most important after short formats?
4. Which agenda fits a board deciding about adoption?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. A three-day or five-day bootcamp.
2. Independent operation of a site; it can show and practise core tasks once.
3. Learners have practised each task only once; the courses help them repeat and go deeper.
4. The [Decision makers' half day](../module-02-one-day-agendas/decision-makers-half-day.md).

</details>
