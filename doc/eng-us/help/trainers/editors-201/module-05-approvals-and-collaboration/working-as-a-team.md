# Lesson 5.3: Working as a team

Two editors on the same article, a colleague on holiday with an unfinished draft, a team that needs to know what
changed: this lesson covers the everyday situations of editorial teams and the tools Exponential has for each.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- resolve the **Possible edit conflict** page: continue an own draft, start a **New draft**, or cancel;
- take over a colleague's draft with **Make a new draft** on the versions page;
- follow a colleague's area with **Add to my notifications** and a digest;
- choose when **Publish without notification** is appropriate;
- agree on three team rules for drafts, naming and handovers.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

Sources: [When someone else is editing](../../../editing-content.md#when-someone-else-is-editing),
[Go back to an earlier version](../../../publishing-and-versions.md#go-back-to-an-earlier-version) and
[E-mail and notifications](../../../accounts-and-sign-in.md#e-mail-and-notifications).

1. **Edit conflicts** (4 minutes). If someone already has a draft of an item, **Edit** shows **Possible edit
   conflict** with the drafts, their creators and times. You can continue one of **your** drafts (select it and press
   **Edit selected**), start a **New draft** from the published version, or **Cancel**. A draft of someone else cannot
   be edited: talk to that person, or make a new draft.
2. **Taking over a draft** (3 minutes). On the versions page, **Make a new draft** works on someone else's draft too:
   the new draft is yours, in the translation you choose. The colleague's draft stays; tell them.
3. **Following an area** (3 minutes). **Add to my notifications** in an item's menu; a digest collects messages per
   day, week or month (**My notification settings**).
4. **Publish without notification** (2 minutes). Where the site offers it, it publishes without e-mailing followers:
   for a typo fix, not for news.
5. **Team rules** (3 minutes). Suggest: discard drafts you will not finish; hand over by telling, not by leaving
   drafts; name items clearly; use **Store draft and exit** with a note in the team chat.

### Common questions

**Can I see who changed what?** The versions page shows each version's creator and dates; administrators also have an
audit trail.

**Will my colleague's draft be overwritten when I publish mine?** No. Their draft stays a draft; when they publish it
later, it becomes the newest version and may undo your change. Talk first.

**How do I know a colleague published in my area?** Follow the area with notifications, or look at **All latest
content** on the dashboard.

### Pitfalls

- **Two drafts, two publishes.** The second publish wins. Make teams talk before editing the same item.
- **Notification overload.** Following the whole site without a digest floods the inbox. Recommend a daily digest.
- **Training mail.** Notifications go nowhere on a training site with `example.invalid` addresses; that is intended.

## Hands-on exercise

Work in pairs (A and B), each with an Editor training account, on an article of A's course folder.

1. A: open the article `Our team, two places`, press **Edit**, change a word and press **Store draft and exit**.
2. B: open the same article and press **Edit**. Read the **Possible edit conflict** page: whose draft is listed?
3. B: press **New draft**, change another word and press **Send for publishing**.
4. A: open the article and press **Edit**. On the conflict page, select your own draft and press **Edit selected**.
   Notice that your draft does not contain B's change.
5. A: press **Discard draft**. Then press **Edit** again to start from B's published version, redo your change, and
   publish.
6. B: open **Manage versions** of the article and read who created each version.
7. B: in the tree, open the item menu of A's course folder and choose **Add to my notifications**. On **My
   notification settings**, set a daily digest and press **Save digest settings**.
8. Both: write three team rules for your own site on a card.

## Expected result

- Step 2 showed A's draft on the conflict page; B published without touching A's draft.
- Step 4 showed that A's old draft lacked B's change; A redid the change on top of B's version.
- The versions page lists versions by both learners.
- B follows A's folder with a daily digest.
- The trainer collects the team rule cards and reads a few aloud.

## Quiz

1. What three choices does **Possible edit conflict** offer?
2. True or false: you can edit a colleague's draft directly.
3. How do you take over a colleague's unfinished draft?
4. When is **Publish without notification** appropriate?
   A. For breaking news B. For a typo fix C. For every publish D. Never
5. Two editors publish drafts made from the same version. Whose text is on the site?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Continue one of your own drafts (**Edit selected**), **New draft**, or **Cancel**
   ([Creating and editing content](../../../editing-content.md#when-someone-else-is-editing)).
2. **False.** Make a new draft from it instead.
3. On the versions page, **Make a new draft** from their draft; the new draft is yours.
4. **B.**
5. The one published last; it may undo the other's change.

</details>
