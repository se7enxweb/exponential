# Module 8: Quality and accessibility

Good content is correct, tidy and usable by everyone. This module teaches the checks that find broken references and
unused files, the writing habits behind accessible pages, and ends with the course's capstone.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Course | [Editors 201](../README.md) |
| Duration | 2 hours 55 minutes (with a 10-minute break) |
| Lessons | 3 |
| Level | Intermediate |

## Lessons

| Lesson | Duration | What learners do |
|---|---|---|
| [Lesson 8.1: Content checks: broken relations and unreferenced files](content-checks.md) | 45 minutes | Find and repair a broken reference, trash an unused image |
| [Lesson 8.2: Accessible content](accessible-content.md) | 60 minutes | Review and fix an article with an accessibility checklist; peer review |
| [Lesson 8.3: Capstone, a multilingual page through approval](capstone-a-multilingual-page.md) | 60 minutes | Produce a complete article alone and in pairs; review against a checklist |

## Preparing this module

- For lesson 8.1: give the learners' role the `contentcheck` policies (unreferenced, trash, brokenrelations) on the
  training installation, or demonstrate. In each course folder, prepare an article that embeds an image which you
  then move to the trash, and an image nobody uses.
- Print the accessibility checklist of lesson 8.2 and the capstone list of lesson 8.3, one per learner.
- Keep the second language, the approval workflow and the tags field of modules 1, 5 and 7 in place for the capstone.
- If the training site uses Exponential Intelligence, decide whether learners may try **Write alt text** (it sends
  the image to a model provider and may cost money); otherwise only show the buttons.
