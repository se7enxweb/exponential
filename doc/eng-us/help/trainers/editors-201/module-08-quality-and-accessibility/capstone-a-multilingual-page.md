# Lesson 8.3: Capstone, a multilingual page through approval

The last lesson of Editors 201 is one task that uses most of the course without step-by-step help. Learners produce
an article that is related, tagged, accessible, translated and approved, and the trainer checks the result against a
list. It is the trainer's own assessment, not an official certification.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, learners can:

- plan and produce a multilingual article without step-by-step instructions;
- combine relations, a second location, tags, a URL alias and accessible writing;
- take an article through approval with a partner;
- check their own work against a list.

## Trainer's notes

### How to present it

1. **The brief** (5 minutes). Project the task list. Learners work in pairs for the approval (each approves the
   other's article) and alone for everything else. They may use the help and their notes.
2. **During the exercise** (45 minutes). Walk around and answer with a question ("Which module covered that?").
   Note common problems.
3. **Review** (10 minutes). Open two articles on the projector and go through the checklist together.

### Common questions

**May I look at the lessons?** Yes.

**What if the approval cronjob is slow?** Run the frequent part yourself or check pending items in the review.

**Is this a certification?** No. It is the trainer's own assessment. The partner program's certifications are
separate and their details are not published ([Certifications](../../../partners/certifications.md)).

### Pitfalls

- **Pairs out of sync.** One partner finishes early and waits to approve. Ask early finishers to review their
  partner's accessibility.
- **Missing permissions** for translations, aliases or tags: test one learner account against the task list before the
  class.

## Hands-on exercise

Use the training installation. Do the tasks in any order.

1. In your course folder, create an article `Capstone: <your topic>` with an intro, a body with two headings in order,
   a list and a link with a meaningful text to another of your articles.
2. Give it a main image with a good alternative text, and embed a second image (decorative or informative: decide and
   write the alternative text accordingly).
3. Tag it with two existing tags.
4. Give it a second location in your `Projects` folder.
5. Translate it into the second language, based on the main language.
6. Create a short URL alias on the site root that redirects to it.
7. Send it for publishing; your partner approves it (or denies it with a comment you then act on).
8. Check it against the accessibility checklist of lesson 8.2.
9. Run **Broken relations in subtree** on your course folder (if your role allows) and fix any finding.

## Expected result

The trainer checks each article against this list:

| Check | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Published in both languages | The badges and the **Translations** tab |
| Headings in order, a list, a meaningful link text | **View on site** |
| Two images with fitting alternative texts | The image items' **View** tabs |
| Two tags | The **Tags** tab |
| Two locations | The **Locations** tab |
| A working short alias | A private window |
| Went through approval | **Manage versions** and the partner's account |
| No broken relations in the course folder | **Broken relations in subtree** (where allowed) |

A learner who meets six of the eight checks has the skills of Editors 201; the trainer suggests the lessons behind
any missing check for review.

## Quiz

1. In which order do you translate and publish when an approval workflow covers both languages?
2. Where do you check that your article has two locations?
3. A partner denied your article with a comment. What do you do next?
4. Name two things the accessibility checklist asks about images.
5. Which page lists the content that points at a removed or trashed item in your folder?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Each translation is published (and approved) on its own; translate, then send each for publishing.
2. On the **Locations** tab.
3. Read the comment, edit the draft and send it for publishing again; a new approval is required.
4. Informative images have an alternative text that says what they show; decorative images have an empty one.
5. **Broken relations** ([The content checks](../../../../features/6.0/content-checks.md)).

</details>
