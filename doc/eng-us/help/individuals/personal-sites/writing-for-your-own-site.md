# Writing for your own site

This page is for individuals who write the pages of their own site. Nobody edits you, so a few habits do the work
an editor would. They apply equally to an About page, a project description and a blog post.

## How people read a page

Most visitors scan: the title, the first sentence, the headings, maybe a picture. Then they decide whether to read
on. They often arrive on a phone, from a search result or a feed, not from your home page. Every page must make
sense on its own.

## Titles

- Say what the page is about, in plain words: "Illustrations for children's books, 2024 to 2026" rather than "Work".
- Keep them short enough to fit a search result and a menu.
- The title becomes the page's address, so a clear title gives a clear address
  ([URL aliases and nice URLs](../../creators/website-creators/url-aliases-and-nice-urls.md)).

## The intro

The *Article* and similar classes have an intro or summary field. It is shown in lists, in feeds and often in search
results. Write it last, in one or two sentences, so that a reader who sees nothing else still learns the point.

## The body

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| One idea per paragraph | Scannable on a phone |
| Headings every few paragraphs | Readers jump; screen readers navigate by headings |
| Lists for steps and options | Easier than long sentences |
| Plain words, short sentences | Readers in a second language thank you |
| Dates where time matters ("as of March 2026") | Pages are read years later |

The rich-text editor keeps headings, lists and links as structure, not as styling
([Editing content](../../editing-content.md)). Use real headings, not bold lines.

## Links and images

- **Link text says where it goes**: "the slides of my 2026 talk", not "click here".
- **Link inside your site with the editor's link tool**, so links follow a page that moves.
- **Every image needs alternative text** that says what the image shows or does. Decorative images can have none.
- **Upload images large enough, not huge**; the site makes smaller copies itself
  ([Image variations for portfolios](../portfolios/image-variations-for-portfolios.md)).
- **Only publish images you may publish**: your own, licensed ones, and photos of people who agreed.

See [Accessibility for site builders](../../creators/website-creators/accessibility-for-site-builders.md) for more.

## Addresses

Pick a title you can live with before you publish; the address follows it. If you rename or move a page later,
Exponential keeps the old address working as a redirect in most cases, and you can add redirects yourself
([Redirects and moved content](../../creators/website-creators/redirects-and-moved-content.md)).

## Updating instead of piling up

A personal site ages quietly. Once a year, reread About, Contact and your project list. When a page is out of date,
update it and add a line at the top saying what changed and when. Remove pages you no longer stand behind; move
them to the trash rather than deleting at once, so you can change your mind
([Archiving and the trash](../../customers/day-to-day/archiving-and-the-trash.md)).

## Your safety net

- **Drafts** are private until you publish.
- **Every publish is a version**; you can compare and go back
  ([Publishing and versions](../../publishing-and-versions.md)).
- **Preview** before publishing shows the page as visitors will see it.
- **Scheduled publishing** lets a page appear later ([Scheduled publishing](../../customers/day-to-day/scheduled-publishing.md)).
- **The writing assistant** can suggest a shorter intro or a clearer title, as a draft you check
  ([The writing assistant](../running-it/writing-assistant-expai.md)).

## Before you press Publish

- [ ] The title says what the page is about.
- [ ] The intro works on its own.
- [ ] Headings, short paragraphs, lists where useful.
- [ ] Every link has meaningful text and works.
- [ ] Every image has alternative text and you may publish it.
- [ ] Nothing personal you would not tell a stranger.
- [ ] Preview looks right on a narrow window.
