# From user to contributor

Every contributor started as a user who noticed something that could be better.

## Noticing

Users see what experienced contributors no longer see: a confusing label, a missing step in a guide, an error that
only happens on their setup. Keep a short list while you work. Each item is a possible contribution.

## The first three contributions

| # | Suggestion | Page |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Something without code: a help page fix, a confirmed bug, a translation | [Your first contribution](../contributing/your-first-contribution.md) |
| 2 | The same kind again, a little larger | [Ways to contribute](../contributing/ways-to-contribute.md) |
| 3 | Something that needs review: a pull request with a test, a new help page, a translated section | [Contributing code](../contributing/contributing-code.md) |

## Choosing one area

Contributors who stay with one area learn it deeply and are trusted there. Examples of areas:

- one language's translation;
- the help pages for one role (editors, administrators);
- one extension you use every day;
- one part of the administration;
- installation on one kind of server or database.

## Building trust

- Finish what you start, or say clearly that you stopped.
- Follow the rules of `CONTRIBUTING.md` without being reminded: prefixed commit messages, the manifest, tests,
  squash and rebase.
- Answer review comments promptly and politely.
- Help newcomers in your area ([Welcoming newcomers](../helping-others/welcoming-newcomers.md)).

## Keeping a record

A list of your merged pull requests, issues and answers is useful for you and for anyone who wants to hire you.
`git shortlog -sn` shows authors in the history; [Tracking your contributions](../../advocates/tracking-your-contributions.md)
shows a simple way to keep your own list.
