## Who maintains Exponential?

7x, a North American company, maintains, develops, supports and hosts Exponential ([Who is 7x?](../../who-is-7x.md)).
That page gives the history of nearly thirty years with this code, with a source for every statement.

## What is the Exponential Foundation?

The nonprofit organization behind the open source project; its page explains how it relates to 7x, the product
family, and the project's support commitment ([Who is the Exponential Foundation?](../../who-is-the-exponential-foundation.md)).
Where its sources are silent, the page says so ([What the sources do not say](../../who-is-the-exponential-foundation.md#what-the-sources-do-not-say)).

## Is the project connected to the former vendors of the code?

No. Exponential continues the code base that eZ Systems developed as eZ Publish, a product its creator discontinued
after almost eighteen years; the project is not associated with eZ Systems or Ibexa
([Not associated with eZ Systems or Ibexa](../../press/not-associated-with-ez-systems-or-ibexa.md)).

## How active is the project?

Check it yourself rather than relying on claims: releases `v6.0.11` to `v6.0.14` were tagged between December 2025
and June 2026, and 6.0.15 is in development ([The roadmap](../../project/roadmap.md#what-has-already-been-delivered));
every version has a changelog ([6.0.15 changelog](../../../../changelogs/6.0/6.0.15.md)); the history records changes since
December 2023 ([the history](../../../history/README.md)). The evaluation part of the procurement section has a method
for judging project health.

## How are decisions made, and can we take part?

The roadmap is public, with a community roadmap for requests ([The roadmap](../../project/roadmap.md)); discussion
happens in the forums and chats ([Community channels](../../project/community-channels.md)); code arrives by pull
request ([Contribute to the project](../../project/contribute-to-the-project.md)). Partners have their own route
([Governance and decisions](../../partners/governance-and-decisions.md)).

## What if the maintainer stops?

The code is GPL and public, with its full history, and the documentation is GFDL. Anyone can continue maintaining it,
including your supplier. This is the protection open source gives that a proprietary license does not; keep your own
copy of the code your site runs and the skills to maintain it ([Exit readiness](../checklists/exit-readiness.md)).
