# Buying training from a partner

This page is for the procurement officer or training coordinator who buys Exponential training from an outside
supplier. It lists what to ask for and how to compare offers. The facts about the partner program come from
[Training partners](../../../partners/training-partners.md) and [Certifications](../../../partners/certifications.md).

## What the program publishes

The Exponential partner program names training centers among its partners and lists "knowledge products (books,
training, certs)" ([Training partners](../../../partners/training-partners.md)). Two things are **not** published,
and you should not assume them:

- **What makes a course "official"**, and who approves trainers: the training partners page says this is still to be
  defined by the Foundation or 7x, and asks partners to describe their courses as their own training about
  Exponential.
- **The content of the certifications** the program names: [Certifications](../../../partners/certifications.md)
  says what is published and what is not yet.

So evaluate a training offer on its outline, its trainer and its results, not on a label.

## What to ask for

| Ask for | Why | Check against |
|---|---|---|
| A course outline with modules, lessons and hours | to compare offers on the same basis | the course overviews in the [Course catalogue](../../../trainers/course-catalogue.md) |
| The Exponential version the course uses | exercises differ between versions | the version your site runs |
| The lab: who provides it, on what | so participants practice on something like your site | [Lab quick card](../../../trainers/lab-quick-card.md) |
| The trainer's experience with Exponential | quality depends on the person | references you check yourself |
| How the course covers accessibility | public sites have legal duties | [Module 8 of Editors 201](../../../trainers/editors-201/module-08-quality-and-accessibility/README.md) |
| Accommodations for participants with disabilities | your duty as an employer | [Accommodations and inclusive practice](../../../trainers/train-the-trainer/module-05-accessibility-and-inclusion/accommodations-and-inclusive-practice.md) |
| Assessment and what a certificate says | honest wording, no claim of official status | [Completion certificates you issue](../../../trainers/assessments/module-01-designing-assessments/completion-certificates-you-issue.md) |
| Material you keep | for onboarding later | see below |
| Course in your site's content model, or generic | tailored training transfers better | your site's classes |

## Comparing offers

Score each offer on the same criteria and weights. A simple sheet:

```text
Criterion                               Weight   Offer A   Offer B
Outline covers the required modules     <w1>     <0-5>     <0-5>
Version and lab match our site          <w2>     ...
Trainer experience (checked references) <w3>
Accessibility in the course             <w4>
Material rights for reuse               <w5>
Price for the defined sessions          <w6>
Weighted total                          sum(weight x score)
```

Compare prices on the same hours. If one offer covers Editors 101 in full and another in a single day, they are not
the same product.

## Rights in the material

The help pages and the trainer courses are free documentation under the GNU FDL 1.3 or later. A supplier that adapts
them must keep the notices and license the adapted material under the same license
([Free documentation](../../../free-documentation.md), [Training partners](../../../partners/training-partners.md)).
In practice this means you may keep, copy and reuse what the supplier hands out when it is built on the help.

Material a supplier writes entirely on its own is under the supplier's terms. Ask in the contract for:

- the right to reuse handouts and exercises for internal onboarding;
- delivery in an editable format;
- material that names your site's own classes and sections, if the course was tailored.

The training clause in the tender templates part of this section has text for this.

## Pitfalls

- **Buying "certification" that the program has not defined.** Ask what exactly the certificate means.
- **No lab.** A course with demonstrations only leaves participants unable to work alone.
- **Course on the vendor's demo site only.** Ask for exercises in your own content model for at least the last day.
- **Forgetting new staff.** Buy the right to run the course again, or plan in-house trainers ("Building an in-house
  training team" in this folder).
