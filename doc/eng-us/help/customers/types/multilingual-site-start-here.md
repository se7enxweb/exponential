# Multilingual sites: start here

A site in several languages is several sites' worth of upkeep. The structure is shared, but every change in one
language raises the question of the others: is the translation still correct, who updates it, and what do readers
see in the meantime? Running a multilingual site well means knowing which language leads, who translates, and how
you notice when a translation has fallen behind.

This page is for the owner of such a site, the translation coordinator and the editors in each language. Planning
a multilingual site is covered in [Multilingual sites for businesses](../../businesses/multilingual/README.md).

## Decide these four things first

1. **The leading language.** Usually one language is written first and the others follow. Write it down; it
   decides what "out of date" means.
2. **Who translates what.** Name a person (or a translation agency through your partner) for each language. Give
   translators a role that lets them translate, not necessarily publish new items
   ([Roles for your team](../day-to-day/roles-for-your-team.md)).
3. **What readers see while a translation is missing.** Your partner can tell you how your site behaves: whether it
   shows the item in another language, or not at all. Agree on it, and tell the editors.
4. **How you notice changes.** The [translation routine](../day-to-day/translation-routine.md) describes a weekly
   look at what changed in the leading language.

> **Tip**
> Machine or assistant translations are drafts. Exponential Intelligence writes drafts only and never publishes on
> its own (`README.md`, "Safety"). Let a person who knows the language read every translation before it is
> published ([Using AI drafts responsibly](../growth/using-ai-drafts-responsibly.md)).

## Adding a language later

A new language is a project, not a setting: addresses, menus, the design, forms, mail and the search all have to
know about it. See [Adding a language](../growth/adding-a-language.md) for what to plan with your partner.
