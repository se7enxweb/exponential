# Blogs: start here

A blog lives from regularity. Readers subscribe because new posts keep coming, and search engines reward a blog
that is kept up. The work is light per post, but it never stops. A blog on Exponential can be run by one person or
by a team of authors, and it can be the whole site or one section of a larger one (a company blog next to the
product pages, for example).

This page is for the blog's owner: the author, the communications lead or the person who answers for the site.

## A blog's rhythm

- **Choose a pace you can keep.** A fixed rhythm (for example, one post a week) is easier for readers and authors
  than bursts. Put it in the [content calendar](../day-to-day/content-calendar.md).
- **Write ahead.** Keep two or three posts as drafts or scheduled, so a busy week does not break the rhythm.
- **Every post**: a clear title, a summary that works on its own (it is what feeds and search results show),
  alternative text for every image, and tags that already exist rather than new spellings of old ones.
- **Every month**: look at the posts that are read and the ones that are not
  ([Analytics and what to measure](../day-to-day/analytics-and-what-to-measure.md)), and at what visitors search for
  ([Site search and what visitors look for](../day-to-day/site-search-and-what-visitors-look-for.md)).

## Comments, if your blog has them

Comments make a blog a conversation and also invite spam. Before you open them, agree with your partner:

- whether comments are published at once or held for review;
- who reviews them and how often;
- what protection the forms have against automated spam
  ([Spam protection for forms](../../creators/website-creators/spam-protection-for-forms.md));
- what you do with a comment that contains personal data someone wants removed
  ([GDPR requests](../commerce/gdpr-requests.md) applies to blog comments as much as to orders).

## A team blog

When several people write, roles keep it clear: authors write and submit, one editor publishes. The editor also
keeps the tag list tidy. See [Roles for your team](../day-to-day/roles-for-your-team.md) and
[Approvals and review](../day-to-day/approvals-and-review.md).

> **Note**
> A post that is out of date is still found by search engines. Rather than deleting it, add a short note at the top
> that says what changed and link to the newer post; delete only what must go, and add a redirect when you move
> something ([URLs, redirects and moved pages](../day-to-day/urls-redirects-and-moved-pages.md)).
