# Onboarding new staff

This page is for the manager who welcomes a new editor, and for the colleague who shows them around. A good first
week gives the person a personal account with the right role, two-step sign-in, a guided first task and the house
rules of your site. The built-in help is the editors' manual: you do not need to write one from scratch
([Handing the site over to editors](../../creators/website-creators/handing-over-to-editors.md)).

## Before day one

- Decide the role and its limits: [Choosing roles for new staff](choosing-roles-for-new-staff.md).
- The account manager creates **one personal account** in the right user group. No shared logins.
- Decide whether two-step sign-in is required for this person; for everyone who can publish it is recommended
  ([Handing the site over to editors](../../creators/website-creators/handing-over-to-editors.md)).
- Prepare a small first task in a safe place: a draft article in their own section, or a page that is hidden.

## Day one

1. **Sign in together.** The new person signs in to the administration ([Logging into the admin](../../logging-into-the-admin.md))
   or, if your site uses it, to the simplified Editor interface ([Logging into the Editor](../../logging-into-the-editor.md)).
2. **Change the password and turn on two-step sign-in** under *My account*
   ([Your account and signing in](../../accounts-and-sign-in.md)).
3. **Take the tour.** Walk through [A tour of the administration](../../admin-tour.md) with your own content.
   A training session that follows the admin tour with the site's own content takes most editors from zero to
   working ([Handing the site over to editors](../../creators/website-creators/handing-over-to-editors.md)).
4. **Make a first change** as a draft, preview it, and publish or discard it
   ([Getting started](../../getting-started.md), [Publishing, versions and the trash](../../publishing-and-versions.md)).

## The first week

| Day | Topic | Help page |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Creating and editing, rich text, relations | [Creating and editing content](../../editing-content.md) |
| 2 | Images and uploads, alternative texts | [Images, media and uploads](../../media-and-uploads.md), [Image and file guidelines](image-and-file-guidelines.md) |
| 3 | Finding things | [Searching](../../searching.md), [Keyboard shortcuts](../../keyboard-shortcuts.md) |

| 4 | Translations, if they translate | [Languages](../../languages.md) |

Show them how to report a problem with the help's **Report an error** page and what to put in it
([When something goes wrong](../../when-something-goes-wrong.md)).

> **Tip**
> Collect the questions new people ask in their first weeks. Repeated questions belong in your site's own editor
> guide ([Handing the site over to editors](../../creators/website-creators/handing-over-to-editors.md)).

## After a month

- Ask what was hard; add it to the guide.
- Check that the role still fits the work. Ask for more access only for real tasks.
- If the person was put behind an approval to learn, decide with the approver whether that stays.

## Who does what

| Step | Who |
|---|---|
| Decide the role | manager, approved by the site owner |
| Create the account, assign the group | account manager or administrator |
| Tour and first task | an experienced editor |
| House rules | editor-in-chief |
| Record the access | account manager, in the [Team roles worksheet](team-roles-worksheet.md) |

The step-by-step list to tick off is the [Onboarding checklist](onboarding-checklist.md).
