# Choosing a partner

You may need a partner because the site is growing, because the current one is leaving, or because you want a second
opinion. This page is the short owner's version for a site that already exists. The full selection process for
businesses is in [Choosing a partner](../../businesses/choosing/choosing-a-partner.md), and the interview questions in
[Questions to ask a partner](../../businesses/choosing/questions-to-ask-a-partner.md).

## What an existing site needs from a partner

A new partner must be able to work with what is already there:

| Need | Ask for evidence |
|---|---|
| Experience with Exponential 6 | Sites they run on the current 6.0 line, and the version of each |
| Ability to read the existing code | A short review of your site's custom parts before a quote |
| Updates on the maintained line | Their routine for releases and security fixes ([Release line and support](../healthy/release-line-and-support.md)) |
| Clean handling of custom code | Code kept in version control; no edits to Exponential's own files without a plan |
| Support terms | Hours, channels, first response per severity, in writing |
| A fair exit | What they hand over if the contract ends ([Handover checklist](handover-checklist.md)) |

## Where to look

From [Choosing a partner](../../businesses/choosing/choosing-a-partner.md):

- the partner program's pages on <https://exponential.earth> and its listing on share.exponential.earth
  ([Partner directories](../../partners/partner-directories.md));
- the community forums and chat: ask who has done similar work;
- 7x directly ([Working with 7x and the project](working-with-7x-and-the-project.md));
- agencies you already know: ask whether they have run Exponential sites, and check the answer.

The program names profiles such as implementation, creative, hosting and distribution partners, with levels from
Student to Platinum. "A level tells you about a company's commitment to the program; it is not a guarantee of
quality for your project" (same page).

## A small paid trial

For an existing site, a good first step is a small, paid, well-defined task: a review of the site with a written
report, or one change request ([Change requests](change-requests.md)). It shows how the partner communicates,
estimates and delivers, before a larger commitment.

## Warning signs

From [Choosing a partner](../../businesses/choosing/choosing-a-partner.md):

- Claims about Exponential you cannot find in its documentation, or invented customers and figures.
- A refusal to hand over the code, the database or administrator access to you, the owner.
- Custom changes inside kernel files with no plan for updates.
- No written answer on security updates, backups or response times.
- A proposal that keeps you on a version older than the maintained 6.0 line.
- Pressure to sign before you have seen the terms on exit and data.

## Bringing a new partner in

- [ ] Give them read access first, not full administrator rights, until the contract is signed.
- [ ] Give them a named personal account with two-step sign-in
      ([Administrator accounts and access](../healthy/admin-accounts-and-access.md)).
- [ ] Share the handover document, if you have one ([Taking over a site](taking-over-a-site.md)).
- [ ] Update [Who does what](who-does-what.md) and your [escalation contacts](escalation-contacts-template.md).
- [ ] Tell the host who the new partner is and what they may do.
