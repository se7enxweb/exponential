# The owner's year at a glance

Owning a site is mostly recurring work. Each piece is small, but it is easy to lose track of which piece is due.
This page puts them on one calendar. The details are in the [checklists](value/checklists/README.md); the
routines of your kind of site are on its start page ([Customer types](types/README.md)).

Copy the calendar into your team's own calendar once, with a named person for each line. The table names who
usually does it; your contracts decide who does it for you
([Who does what](partners-and-hosts/who-does-what.md)).

## The rhythm

| How often | What | Usually done by | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every day | Is the site up; what waits for approval; forms and orders that came in | Site manager, store manager | [Daily checklist](value/checklists/daily.md), [Daily store routine](commerce/daily-store-routine.md) |
| Every week | Content that needs attention, broken links, accounts, searches without results | Site manager | [Weekly checklist](value/checklists/weekly.md), [Weekly owner routine](day-to-day/weekly-owner-routine.md) |
| Every month | Releases and updates, backups, security signals, costs | Owner with host and partner | [Monthly checklist](value/checklists/monthly.md) |
| Every quarter | A restore test, an access review, a content audit, costs against budget | Owner, host | [Quarterly checklist](value/checklists/quarterly.md) |
| Every year | The value review, contracts, release line and PHP, data protection, next year's plan | Owner | [Yearly checklist](value/checklists/yearly.md) |

## Things that are not on a fixed date

Some work follows events rather than the calendar:

| When | What | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Someone joins the team | Account and roles ready on the first day | [Onboarding new staff](day-to-day/onboarding-new-staff.md) |
| Someone leaves | Account disabled on the last day | [Offboarding staff](day-to-day/offboarding-staff.md) |
| A campaign, a sale or a launch is coming | Landing page, mail, capacity, a change freeze | [Before a campaign](value/checklists/before-a-campaign.md) |
| A new Exponential release is out | Plan the upgrade with your provider | [How updates work](healthy/how-updates-work.md) |
| Something went wrong | Notes, cause, follow-up | [After an incident](value/checklists/after-an-incident.md) |
| A customer or member asks for their data | Record, answer within the time your adviser names | [GDPR requests](commerce/gdpr-requests.md) |
| Your partner or host changes | Handover | [Handover checklist](partners-and-hosts/handover-checklist.md) |

## A year, quarter by quarter

A simple way to spread the larger pieces so they do not all land in the same month:

| Quarter | Larger piece of work | Page |
|---|---|---|
| First | The yearly review of value and plans, agreed budget for the year | [Annual value review](value/annual-value-review.md) |
| Second | A full restore test together with the host | [Restore test](healthy/restore-test.md) |
| Third | The yearly provider review, before contracts renew | [Reviewing your provider each year](partners-and-hosts/reviewing-your-provider-each-year.md) |
| Fourth | Accessibility and content audit of the whole site, preparation for peak season | [Accessibility upkeep](day-to-day/accessibility-upkeep.md), [Peak season preparation](commerce/peak-season-preparation.md) |

This order is a suggestion, not a rule. Move the pieces to the months that suit your organization: a store may want
the restore test well before its busiest season, a school before the start of the year.

> **Tip**
> Put the next date of each yearly item in writing at the end of the current one. A restore test that is "done
> sometime next year" tends not to happen.
