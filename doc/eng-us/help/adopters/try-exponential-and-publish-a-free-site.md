# Try Exponential and publish a free site

Evaluating a CMS usually means paying for hosting before you know whether you will keep it. With static publishing,
your pilot site can be public from day one at no cost: Exponential runs on a laptop or a test server, and the public
copy is on GitHub Pages.

## What you'll have at the end

- A pilot site your stakeholders can open at a real address.
- First-hand experience of Exponential's editing, roles and versions.
- A clear answer to "could our public site be static?"

## A one-week pilot

| Day | Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Install Exponential with a demo package; see [Adopters: start here](adopters-start-here.md) |
| 2 | Model two or three content types you really have; add real content |
| 3 | Pick or adapt a design |
| 4 | Make a static publishing target and publish to GitHub Pages |
| 5 | Let editors work for a day; publish again; try a rollback; collect feedback |

## What to evaluate

- **Editing:** can your editors do their daily work without training?
- **The static copy:** is everything your visitors need there? Check the build's report for what was degraded
  (search, forms, login).
- **Fit:** [Is static right for my site?](../static-publishing/is-static-right-for-my-site.md) If not, Exponential
  serves the site dynamically as well; static publishing is an option, not a requirement.

## After the pilot

- Keep the static site and move the editing installation to its final place, or
- move to a dynamic public site, with the static copy as a backup or a status page.

Either way, the content you entered stays.
