# Rolling out to editors

Editors decide whether an adoption succeeds. They need the new system to make their work easier, and they need to
feel heard.

## Contents

1. [Champions](#champions)
2. [Training](#training)
3. [Guidelines](#guidelines)
4. [Support in the first weeks](#support-in-the-first-weeks)
5. [Later staff](#later-staff)

## Champions

Choose one or two editors early, involve them in the proof of concept and the content model, and let them help
their colleagues at launch.

## Training

- Train one or two weeks before launch, on the new site with real content
  ([Skills and training](../planning/skills-and-training.md)).
- Use short sessions per task: create, edit, publish, images, translations.
- Course material exists in the trainers' section, for example [Editors 101](../../trainers/editors-101/README.md).
- Show the help: the "?" in the administration opens pages for the screen in front of them.

## Guidelines

Write one or two pages for your team:

- which content type to use for what;
- image sizes and alternative texts ([Image and file guidelines](../../customers/day-to-day/image-and-file-guidelines.md));
- who approves what ([Approvals and review](../../customers/day-to-day/approvals-and-review.md));
- accessibility basics ([Accessibility checklist for editors](../../customers/day-to-day/accessibility-checklist-for-editors.md)).

## Support in the first weeks

| Measure | Why |
|---|---|
| Daily office hours in the first week | Questions get answered while they are fresh |
| One channel for questions | Nothing gets lost |
| A list of questions and answers | Becomes your internal FAQ |
| A review after a month | Second training on real problems |
