# Is static right for my site?

## What you'll have at the end

A yes, a no, or a "yes, for part of it", in five minutes.

## The checklist

Answer each question for the public part of your site.

| Question | Static suits it when |
|---|---|
| Do visitors mostly **read** and look? | yes |
| Does the content change a few times a day or less? | yes (a build takes minutes, not seconds) |
| Do visitors need to **sign in** to see things? | no |
| Do visitors **buy** or pay on the site? | no |
| Are forms the heart of the site (bookings, applications)? | no; one contact form is fine |
| Is the site under 1 GB with no single file over 100 MB? | yes, for GitHub Pages ([limits](github-pages-limits-and-terms.md)) |
| May all of it be public? | yes, or you leave the rest out ([Keep private content private](keep-private-content-private.md)) |

## Good fits

- A **brochure site** for a business, a club or a practice.
- A **blog** or a news section.
- **Documentation** and handbooks.
- A **portfolio** or a personal site.
- An **event** or conference site.
- A **nonprofit**, a school, a community group or a public information site.

## Poor fits

- A **shop**: GitHub's terms rule it out, and a basket needs a server.
- A **members' area** or intranet: whatever is behind a login is never in the copy.
- A site that lives on **forms** and search results, such as booking or directory sites.
- Content that changes every minute (live scores, stock levels).

## Part of a site

A mixed site can still use it: publish the public pages statically and leave the rest on your own hosting. Leave
pages out by path or section (step 7 of the wizard); links to them go to your live site.
