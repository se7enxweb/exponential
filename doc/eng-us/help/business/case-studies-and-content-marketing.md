# Writing case studies and content marketing

This page is for people in sales and marketing who want to tell the story of a real Exponential project, and for
the editors who publish those stories. It answers: how do you collect, write, approve and publish a case study
without ever stating something that is not true or not agreed, and how do you run content marketing with the tools
Exponential already has?

> **Warning**
> This help contains **no** customer names and no case studies, on purpose: the sources of this documentation do not
> name any customer. Never invent a customer, a quote, a number or a result. A case study exists only when a real
> organization has agreed, in writing, to the exact text you publish.

## What a good case study is

A case study tells one organization's problem, what it did with Exponential, and what changed, in a way the
organization itself agrees with. It persuades because it is specific and true.

| A good case study | A bad case study |
|---|---|
| Names a problem in the customer's words | Starts with product features |
| Says which parts of Exponential were used, with links to their help pages | Claims features the product does not have |
| Gives results the customer measured and agreed to publish | Gives numbers nobody can verify |
| Has the customer's written approval of the final text | Was "cleared on the phone" |
| Says when it was written and which version was used | Is undated and never reviewed |

## The process at a glance

| Step | Who leads | Output | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Choose a candidate | Sales or project manager | A short list | The project is live and the customer relationship is good |
| 2. Consent to participate | Sales | Written consent to be approached and interviewed | **No consent, no case study** |
| 3. Interview and facts | Marketing writer | Notes, the facts sheet | Every number has a source in the customer's own data |
| 4. Draft | Marketing writer | Draft in the template below | Uses only the facts sheet |
| 5. Fact check | Project manager and a developer | Checked draft | Every product statement links to a help page |
| 6. Written approval | Customer contact with authority | Signed-off final text | **No approval, no publication** |
| 7. Publish | Editor | Published object in Exponential | Approval workflow passed |
| 8. Maintain | Marketing | Review date, withdrawal on request | Withdrawal is honored at once |

Steps 2 and 6 are the consent and approval steps. They are not optional, and they are not the same thing: step 2
is agreement to take part, step 6 is agreement to the exact words.

## Step 1: choose a candidate

Questions to answer for each candidate project:

1. Is the site live, and has it been running long enough for results to be visible?
2. Did the project solve a problem that other prospects also have (several languages, several sites, a shop,
   editorial approval, migration from a site built in the eZ Publish years)?
3. Is the customer relationship good, and is there a contact who can approve publications for their organization?
4. Did the project use features you can link to in this help? (See [Feature highlights mapped to customer needs](feature-highlights-for-customers.md).)
5. Does the customer have any reason to stay anonymous (security, contracts, competition)?

## Step 2: ask for consent before you write

Ask first, in writing, before any interview. Your request should say:

- what you want to publish (a case study, a quote, a logo, screenshots) and where (website, PDF, newsletter, slides);
- that nothing will be published without their written approval of the final text;
- that they may withdraw the case study later, and how;
- what personal data you will process (the names and job titles of the people interviewed, if they agree to be
  named) and that they can choose to be quoted without a name.

```text
Subject: May we tell the story of <project name>?

Dear <contact>,

we would like to write a short case study about <project name> for <where it will appear>.

What we would publish: <case study text | a quote | your logo | screenshots of the public site>.
How it works: we interview <who> for about <duration>, write a draft, and send it to you.
Nothing is published until you approve the final text in writing.
You may ask us to change or withdraw it at any time; write to <address>.
You decide whether people are named, or quoted by role only.

May we go ahead?

<your name>, <your organization>
```

> **Note**
> This page is not legal advice. Whether you need more than this (a written release, a data processing agreement,
> rules for the people quoted) depends on your country and contracts. Ask your own legal adviser, and record the
> answer in your process.

Keep the consent with the case study's file. If your organization keeps customer records in Exponential, never put
the consent documents or personal data in a published part of the content tree; use a section that only the
responsible roles may read ([Roles and policies](../../guides/roles-and-policies.md), [Sections](../../guides/sections.md)).

## Step 3: interview and collect facts

Interview questions that produce usable material:

1. What was the situation before the project? What did not work?
2. Why did you choose Exponential? Which alternatives did you consider? (Record the answer, but do not publish
   comparisons with named products without checking [Comparing Exponential factually](comparing-exponential-factually.md).)
3. Which parts of the product do your editors use every day?
4. What changed after launch? How do you know? Which figure, from which system, for which period?
5. What would you tell another organization in your position?
6. What was hard, and how was it solved?

Build a **facts sheet** while you listen. Every line has a source.

| # | Fact | Source (customer data, document, help page) | May be published? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `<fact>` | `<source>` | yes / no / ask |
| 2 | `<fact>` | `<source>` | yes / no / ask |

Results come only from the customer's own measurements. For figures that Exponential itself records, such as the
audit trail or request logs, ask a project manager which ones the product can actually produce
([Measuring a project](../leadership/kpis-from-logs-and-audit.md)).

## Step 4: write the draft

Write only from the facts sheet. Rules:

- One problem, one solution, one set of results. Short paragraphs.
- Product statements link to the help page that proves them. Use the product names exactly as
  [Brand and naming rules](brand-and-naming-rules.md) writes them.
- Quotes are the customer's words, as approved. Never improve a quote without approval of the new wording.
- Say the Exponential version and the date. A case study about 6.0.14 must not suggest a 6.0.15 feature was used.
- Exponential Intelligence can help write a first draft from your facts sheet: it "writes drafts, as the signed-in
  editor and with that editor's permissions" and "never publishes on its own"
  ([Exponential Intelligence](../../features/6.0/extensions/expai.md)). The facts check and the approval still apply
  to every word it suggests.

## Step 5: check every fact

| Check | Who | How |
|---|---|---|
| Every product statement is true for the version used | Developer or administrator | Compare with the feature page and the [changelogs](../../../changelogs/README.md) |
| Every number is on the facts sheet with a source | Writer | Line by line |
| No personal data beyond what was agreed | Writer and sales | Names, photos, e-mail addresses removed unless approved |
| No security details | Administrator | No host names, versions of server software, internal addresses or screenshots of settings |
| Names and spelling | Editor | [Brand and naming rules](brand-and-naming-rules.md) |
| Screenshots show only test or public data | Editor | No real user names, no real customer records in the admin |

## Step 6: written approval of the final text

Send the final text, exactly as it will appear, with every quote, image and caption. Ask for approval in writing
from a person who can approve for their organization. Record:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Case study | `<title>` |
| Version of the text | `<date or version number>` |
| Approved by (role) | `<role, not necessarily a name in your public files>` |
| Date of approval | `<date>` |
| Channels approved | `<website, PDF, newsletter, slides ...>` |
| Named people approved | yes / no |
| Logo approved | yes / no |
| Review date | `<date, for example one year later>` |

Any change after approval, even a "small" one, goes back to the customer.

Exponential gives you the controls to make the approval step technical as well as organizational.

| Need | How in Exponential | Read |
|---|---|---|
| A structured case study | A content class with the fields of the template below (title, customer, problem, solution, results, quote, version, date) | [The content model and editing content](../../guides/content-model-and-editing.md) |
| Nobody publishes without a second person | An approval workflow before publishing | [Workflows](../../guides/workflows.md#3-worked-example-approval-before-publishing) |

## Step 8: maintain and withdraw

- Put a review date on every case study (one year is a reasonable default; set your own).
- At review: is the customer still using the site? Is the version statement still accurate? Is the contact still
  happy with it?
- A withdrawal request is honored at once: hide the item, then remove it, and remove it from feeds, newsletters,
  PDFs and slides you control. Tell the customer when it is done.

## The case study template

Copy this into your content class or your word processor.

```text
TITLE:          <customer or anonymous description> <does what> with Exponential
CUSTOMER:       <organization name, or "a <sector> organization in <region>">
SECTOR:         <sector>
WEBSITE:        <public address, only if approved>
VERSION USED:   Exponential <6.0.x>   DATE WRITTEN: <date>   REVIEW BY: <date>

IN SHORT (3 lines)
- The problem: <one sentence>
- What they did: <one sentence>
- The result: <one sentence, with an approved figure only>

THE SITUATION BEFORE
<2-4 short paragraphs in the customer's terms>

WHY EXPONENTIAL
<the customer's reasons, as they stated them>

WHAT THEY BUILT
- <feature used> - see <help page>
- <feature used> - see <help page>
- <extension used> - see <help page>

THE RESULTS
| Measure | Before | After | Period | Source |
| <measure> | <value> | <value> | <period> | <customer system> |

IN THEIR WORDS
"<approved quote>" - <name and title, or role only>

WHO WORKED ON IT
<customer team, agency, 7x or other partners, only as approved>

APPROVAL RECORD (internal, not published)
Consent to participate: <date>   Final text approved: <date>   Channels: <list>
```

### Topics that stay true

Good topics for an Exponential content program are the ones the documentation can prove:

- "How we publish in <n> languages from one installation" (multilingual, [Languages in Exponential](../../languages.md));
- "Our editorial approval, step by step" ([Workflows](../../guides/workflows.md));
- "Building pages without a developer" ([The layouts editor](../layouts-editor.md));
- "What changed in Exponential <version>" (from the [changelogs](../../../changelogs/README.md));
- "Why we keep our site in Git" ([Develop with Git](../develop-with-git.md));
- "How the help inside the product works" ([The help system](../../features/6.0/help-system.md)).

When you write about the product, you may reuse the help pages themselves: they are free documentation under the
GNU FDL 1.3, provided you follow its conditions ([Free documentation](../free-documentation.md)). Quoting a short
passage with its source is the simplest way.
