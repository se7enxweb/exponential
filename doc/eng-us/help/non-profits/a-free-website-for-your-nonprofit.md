# A free website for your nonprofit

Every euro or dollar you spend on hosting is one you do not spend on your cause. With static publishing, your
volunteers edit the site in Exponential as usual, and the public site is a copy of plain HTML pages hosted for free on
GitHub Pages.

## What you'll have at the end

- A public website at `https://<your-account>.github.io/<repository>/` or at your own domain, with HTTPS.
- No hosting fee, no server to patch, nothing on the public side that can be hacked into.
- Every publish kept, so a mistake is undone with one click.

## Why it suits a nonprofit

- **No running costs.** GitHub Pages hosts public sites for free. A domain name is the only thing you may pay for.
- **Safe with volunteers.** Editors never touch the public site directly. They edit, someone checks the preview, then
  it is published. A wrong publish is rolled back from the history.
- **Fast everywhere.** Plain files load quickly, on old phones and slow connections too.
- **Stays up.** A news mention or a campaign day does not take the site down.

## Where it is not enough

A static site cannot take payments or keep a members area. Keep those on a service made for them and link to it:

- **Donations:** link to your payment provider's donation page.
- **Contact and sign-up forms:** they can post to your Exponential installation, or show a link to the live page. See
  [Search, forms and comments on a static site](../static-publishing/search-forms-and-comments-on-a-static-site.md).
- **Members only pages:** they stay on the Exponential site and are never copied. See
  [Keep private content private](../static-publishing/keep-private-content-private.md).

Not sure? [Is static right for my site?](../static-publishing/is-static-right-for-my-site.md) has a short checklist.

## Who does what

| Role | Task |
|---|---|
| Editors | Write and update pages in Exponential, as before |
| One administrator | Sets up the target once with the wizard, publishes or switches on automatic publishing |
| Your board | Owns the GitHub account and the domain, so the site never depends on one volunteer |

Create the GitHub account in the organization's name, with two people who can sign in.
