# Accessibility statement for non-profits

This page is for the communications lead or director who signs the statement. An accessibility statement tells
visitors how accessible your site is, what you know does not work, and how to reach you if something blocks them.
Public bodies are often required to publish one; for a non-profit it is good practice and sometimes a funder's
condition. The detailed guide is [Writing an accessibility statement](../../government/accessibility/writing-an-accessibility-statement.md).

## What it contains

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Commitment | That you want everyone to be able to use the site |
| Standard | The standard you measure against, for example WCAG 2.2 level AA |
| Status | Your honest assessment: fully, partly or not conforming, based on testing you actually did |
| Known issues | What does not work, and the alternative meanwhile |
| Feedback | How to report a problem and get the content another way |
| Method and date | How and when you tested; when you will review |

## Describing the software honestly

Your statement is about **your site**. If you mention the software, use the project's own words and do not upgrade
them ([What Exponential provides](../../government/accessibility/what-exponential-provides.md)):

- The admin4 administration is designed and regression-tested against WCAG 2.2 AA; the project rates it
  "Partly complies" and makes no conformance claim.
- There is no VPAT, no Accessibility Conformance Report and no WCAG-EM report for Exponential.
- Public designs are not audited by the project; your public design is your responsibility.

Never write "our CMS is WCAG certified". If you need a formal report, see
[Preparing an ACR](../../government/accessibility/preparing-an-acr.md).

## Known issues

List them plainly, with what you do about them. Typical entries for a non-profit site:

| Issue | Alternative meanwhile | Planned fix |
|---|---|---|
| Older PDF annual publications are not tagged | Summaries on HTML pages; send on request | Accessible PDFs from next year |
| Some older videos lack captions | Transcript on request | Captions by a date |
| The CAPTCHA on the volunteer form may be hard to solve | Apply by mail or phone | Review provider settings |
| The embedded donation form (if used) has not been tested by us | The Donate link opens the platform's own page | Test, or replace by a link |

Write your own list from your own tests ([Accessibility basics for non-profits](accessibility-basics-for-non-profits.md#testing-without-a-budget)).

## Feedback and response

- A contact form or address on the statement page, and a link to the statement in every page footer.
- Promise a response time you can keep.
- Track what people report and what you fixed ([Monitoring and feedback](../../government/accessibility/monitoring-and-feedback.md)).
- A contact form is built with the information collector ([Building a contact form](../../creators/website-creators/building-a-contact-form.md)).

## An outline to fill in

```text
Accessibility statement for [site address]

[Organization] wants as many people as possible to be able to use this website.

Standard: We aim to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA.

Status: This website is [fully / partly / not] conforming. We last tested it on [date]
by [method: keyboard testing, automated checks, testing with users, outside audit].

What does not work yet:
- [issue] - meanwhile [alternative] - we plan to fix it by [date]

Feedback: If you cannot use part of this site, contact [address / form / phone].
We reply within [days].

This statement was prepared on [date] and will be reviewed by [date].
```

## Keeping it current

- Review it every year and after a redesign ([Annual checklist](../checklists/annual-checklist.md)).
- Keep it in a staff-only section so volunteers do not change it by accident
  ([Volunteers as editors](../volunteers/volunteers-as-editors.md#pages-only-staff-may-change)).
- Versions keep the history of the statement ([Versions and the audit trail](../transparency/versions-and-the-audit-trail.md)).
