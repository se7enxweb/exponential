# The advocacy ladder: user, advocate, leader

This page is for advocates who want to know where they stand and what comes next, and for leaders who help others
climb. It answers: what are the rungs, what does a person do on each, and what is the one next step from each?

The ladder builds on the contributor funnel of [Growing the community](../business/growing-the-community.md#the-contributor-funnel)
and the contribution ladder of [Becoming a leader](../leadership/becoming-a-community-leader.md#the-contribution-ladder).
It adds the advocate's view: helping **other people** climb.

## The ladder at a glance

| Rung | Who | What they do | Next step | Page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. User | Runs, edits or builds a site | Uses the help, asks questions | Answer one question for someone else | [Getting started](../getting-started.md) |
| 2. Helper | Answers questions now and then | Shares links to help pages | Write or present something | [Answering questions well](answering-questions-well.md) |
| 3. Advocate | Talks, writes, demonstrates | Brings new people in, with sources | Organize or mentor | [Talking points: the core set](talking-points-core-set.md) |
| 4. Organizer and mentor | Runs meetups, sprints, mentors first contributions | Helps others become advocates | Own an area | [Mentoring a first contribution](mentoring-a-first-contribution.md) |
| 5. Leader | Owns an area: a language, an extension, a channel, a topic | Keeps it healthy over time | Grow the next leader | [Becoming a leader](../leadership/becoming-a-community-leader.md) |

The rungs are a way to think, not titles. The project does not award them; the sources name no ranks or programs.

## Rung 1: user

**Who.** Someone who signs in to the administration, edits content, builds a site or runs a server.

**What helps them.** The help (the "?" in the header), the [help for editors and users](../README.md), the
[forums](https://share.exponential.earth/forums) and [Telegram](https://t.me/exponentialcms).

**The next step.** Answer one question you know the answer to. You already know more than someone who started
yesterday.

## Rung 2: helper

**Who.** A user who answers a question now and then.

**Habits.**

- Answer with a link to a help page or a documentation page, not from memory alone.
- Say what you tried and what version you use.
- Thank people who answer you.

**The next step.** Write one thing down: a short post about what you learned, a correction to a help page with
**Edit on GitHub** ([Improving the help](improving-the-help.md)), or a five-minute demo for a colleague.

## Rung 3: advocate

**Who.** A helper who now also talks about Exponential in public: posts, talks, demos, social media.

**Habits.**

- Uses the [talking points](talking-points-core-set.md) and cites the source of every claim.
- Uses the right names ([Naming rules quick card](naming-rules-quick-card.md)).
- Says who they are and whether anyone pays them ([Disclosure](disclosure-and-conflicts-of-interest.md)).
- Sends people to the documented channels only ([The community channels](community-channels.md)).

**The next step.** Help someone else start: welcome a newcomer, review a draft post of another advocate, or propose
a small meetup.

## Rung 4: organizer and mentor

**Who.** An advocate who makes it easier for others to contribute.

**What they do.**

| Activity | Page |
|---|---|
| Propose and run a meetup | [Proposing a meetup](proposing-a-meetup.md), [Running an online meetup](running-an-online-meetup.md) |
| Run a documentation or translation sprint | [Proposing a contribution sprint](proposing-a-contribution-sprint.md) |
| Mentor a first pull request | [Mentoring a first contribution](mentoring-a-first-contribution.md) |
| Welcome newcomers in the channels | [Welcoming newcomers](welcoming-newcomers.md) |

**The next step.** Choose one area you will look after for a long time.

## Rung 5: leader

**Who.** Someone who owns an area: a translation, an extension, a section of the documentation, a channel, a local
community, a topic such as accessibility.

**What changes.** A leader thinks in years. They write things down so others can continue, they review others' work,
and they grow the next leader. The full description, with a 30/60/90-day plan, is on
[Becoming a leader in the Exponential community](../leadership/becoming-a-community-leader.md).

**Documented openings for leaders.**

- Translations: ten administration translations are complete; for every other language, translating the strings
  added since 6.0 is "the most useful work" ([Languages in Exponential, section 8](../../languages.md#8-adding-or-completing-an-administration-translation)).
- Documentation: every help page can be improved with **Edit on GitHub** ([Free documentation](../free-documentation.md#help-others)).
- Extensions: an extension states its version in its own files and is released as a Composer package
  ([Extensions, Release it](../../guides/extensions.md#7-release-it)).

## Moving people up

| They are on | Invite them to | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 1. User | Answer a question | "You solved this last week. Someone in the forum has the same problem." |
| 2. Helper | Write it down | "That answer was great. Would you add it to the help page? I can show you how." |
| 3. Advocate | Mentor | "A newcomer asked about translations. Would you guide them through their first pull request?" |
| 4. Organizer | Own an area | "You ran three sprints for your language. Would you look after it?" |

Rules:

- Invite, never push. Volunteers give the time they have.
- Credit people publicly for what they did, with their consent ([Recognition ideas](recognition-ideas.md)).
- Make the first step small and clearly described.

## Stepping back is fine

Life changes. An advocate who steps back should:

1. Tell the people who rely on them.
2. Write down what they know (a short handover note in the channel or a help page).
3. Point to someone who can continue, if there is one.

Nobody owes the project their time. A good handover is a final contribution.
