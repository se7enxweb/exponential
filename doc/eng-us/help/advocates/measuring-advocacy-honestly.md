# Measuring advocacy honestly

This page is for advocates who want to know if what they do helps. The honest answer is often "partly, and hard to
prove". Measure what you can observe, never invent numbers, and never track people.

## Contents

1. [Signals you can observe](#signals-you-can-observe)
2. [What not to measure](#what-not-to-measure)
3. [A quarterly review](#a-quarterly-review)

## Signals you can observe

| Signal | Where |
|---|---|
| Questions you answered that were marked solved or thanked | The forums |
| The same question stops repeating after you improved a help page | The forums, the chat |
| Your pull requests merged | GitHub |
| Newcomers you helped who made a first contribution | GitHub (public) |
| Translation progress of your language | `ezchecktranslation.php` ([Languages, section 8](../../languages.md#8-adding-or-completing-an-administration-translation)) |
| Issues you confirmed that got fixed | GitHub |
| People at your meetups returning | Your own count, no names |

[Growing the community](../business/growing-the-community.md#measuring-what-you-can-actually-see) lists similar
measures for organizations.

## What not to measure

- Followers and likes as proof of anything: they measure reach, not help.
- "Installations gained": nobody can count them, and the sources do not.
- Anything that needs tracking individual people without their knowledge.

## A quarterly review

```text
QUARTER <Q/year>
Did:            <links from my contribution log>
Helped most:    <which, and the signal>
Did not work:   <what, why>
All claims sourced?  <yes / corrections made: links>
Next quarter:   <1-3 goals>
```
