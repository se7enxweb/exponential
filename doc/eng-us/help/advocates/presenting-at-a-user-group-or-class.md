# Presenting at a user group or class

This page is for advocates who want to present Exponential to an existing group: a local PHP user group, a web
developers' meetup, a university course or a vocational school class. These groups already have an audience; your job
is to offer something useful to **them**, on their terms.

## Finding a slot

- Ask the organizers of groups you already attend. Being a regular helps more than any pitch.
- Read the group's rules: many forbid sales talks. As an advocate you are not selling; say so.
- For classes, contact the teacher with a topic that fits their curriculum: content modeling, open source
  collaboration, accessibility, PHP application architecture, internationalization.

## The proposal

```text
Subject: Talk proposal: <title>

Hello <organizer>,

I'd like to propose a <length>-minute talk for <group>: "<title>".

What it is about: <two sentences, what attendees learn, not what you sell>.
Who it is for: <level>.
Format: <talk | talk + live demo | hands-on>.

About me: I use Exponential for <your use>. I'm a volunteer; nobody pays me for this talk.
<If someone does pay you, say who.>

Everything I show is free software (GNU GPL) and the material is free documentation.
I can share the slides afterwards.

Thank you,
<name>
```

Title ideas that teach rather than sell:

- "Content modeling without code: lessons from a 25-year-old PHP CMS"
- "Keeping an open source project alive after its vendor left"
- "Right-to-left in a CMS administration: what it takes"
- "A PHP web server written in PHP"
- "How a CMS documents itself: help inside the application"

## Teaching with Exponential

Teachers may reuse the documentation: it is free under the GNU FDL, so pages can be printed, copied into course
material and translated, under the license's conditions ([Free documentation](../free-documentation.md)). Useful
starting points:

| For students who | Use |
|---|---|
| Never used a CMS | [Getting started](../getting-started.md), [A tour of the administration](../admin-tour.md) |
| Learn PHP | [Extensions](../../guides/extensions.md), [Templates and design](../../guides/templates-and-design.md) |
| Learn Git and open source | [Develop with Git](../develop-with-git.md) |
| Learn about software history | [The history](../../history/README.md) |
| Want a small CMS to study | Exponential Basic, "for everyone, educational purposes, personal use" ([Exponential Basic](../who-is-the-exponential-foundation.md#exponential-basic)) |

Students' first contributions are welcome: a documentation fix or a translation is a real pull request
([Mentoring a first contribution](mentoring-a-first-contribution.md)). Do not ask a class to flood a project with
practice pull requests; agree on real, small tasks first.

## On the day

- Arrive early; test the projector and the network.
- Start with your disclosure: who you are, why you care.
- Keep a demo backup as screenshots ([Live demo safety](live-demo-safety.md)).
- Leave a third of the time for questions.

## Follow-up

1. Post the slides and the links within a day.
2. Answer the questions you could not answer, with sources, in the group's channel or the forum.
3. Thank the organizers publicly.
4. Note in your [contribution log](tracking-your-contributions.md) what you presented, where and when; no attendee
   names.
