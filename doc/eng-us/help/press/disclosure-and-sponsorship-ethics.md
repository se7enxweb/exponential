# Disclosure and sponsorship ethics

This page is for every creator who makes content about Exponential: videos, streams, podcasts, posts, newsletters.
Your audience trusts you because they believe you speak from your own experience. If money, a gift, a job or a
business tie stands behind what you say, they deserve to know before they decide what to make of it.

> **Important**
> This is ethical guidance, not legal advice. Laws and platform rules about marking paid or sponsored content differ
> from country to country and change over time. Check the rules where you live and where you publish, and the rules
> of each platform you use. The documentation of Exponential cites no specific law, and neither does this page.

The advocate's version of this page is [Disclosure and conflicts of interest](../advocates/disclosure-and-conflicts-of-interest.md);
this page adds what is specific to creators.

## What counts as a tie

| Your situation | Disclose? |
|---|---|
| Nobody paid you or gave you anything | Not required; "nobody paid for this video" helps viewers |
| You were paid for this content, by anyone | Yes, clearly, at the start |
| You received something for free: hosting, a license, an extension, travel, equipment | Yes, and say what |
| You work for 7x, or are paid by the Exponential Foundation | Yes, always |
| You work for, own or advise a company that sells Exponential services, hosting, themes or extensions | Yes, always |
| Your links earn a commission, or your company earns from a referral | Yes ([Paid, gifted and affiliate content](paid-gifted-and-affiliate-content.md)) |
| You show your own extension, theme or course | Yes |
| You sponsor 7x or the Foundation on GitHub | Optional; it is not a payment to you, but openness is welcome |
| A client asked you to show their site | Yes, and only with their written consent ([Privacy on camera](privacy-on-camera.md)) |

When you are unsure, disclose. A disclosure nobody needed costs nothing; a hidden one, found later, costs the
audience's trust.

## Where to put the disclosure

Put it where people see or hear it **before** the content can persuade them, and in a form the medium keeps:

| Medium | Where |
|---|---|
| Video | Said aloud in the first seconds and shown on screen; again in the first lines of the description |
| Live stream | Said at the start and repeated when new viewers arrive; in the stream title or panel |
| Podcast | Said at the start of the episode, before the segment, and in the show notes |
| Blog post or newsletter | Above the first paragraph, not only in a footer |
| Social post | In the post text itself, not only in a linked page or an image |
| Short-form video | Said or shown on screen at the start, and in the caption |

Use the platform's own "paid promotion" or "sponsored" marking where it exists, **as well as** your own words. Say it
in the language of your content.

## Disclosure lines to copy

```text
NO TIE
Nobody paid for this video, and I received nothing for it.

PAID
This video was paid for by <who>. They <did | did not> see it before it was published.

GIFT
<Who> gave me <what> for free. They asked for nothing in return, and I said what I think.

EMPLOYMENT
I work for <company>, which <sells Exponential services | offers hosting | makes extensions>.

7x
I work for 7x, the company that leads the development of Exponential.

COMMISSION
Some links below earn me or my company a commission. You pay the same either way.

OWN PRODUCT
I made <extension | theme | course>, which I show in this video.
```

## Editorial control

- Decide before you accept anything whether the sponsor may see the content first, and say so in the disclosure.
- A sponsor may correct a factual error; a sponsor may not turn your opinion into theirs. If they insist, say so or
  decline.
- Never present a sponsor's text as your own words. Quote it as theirs.
- 7x and the Exponential Foundation are two organizations; never say a video was "made with" or "approved by" either
  unless they told you so in writing ([Brand and naming rules](../business/brand-and-naming-rules.md#the-nonprofit-the-exponential-foundation)).
- The press kit's usage guidelines say: "Do not use assets to imply endorsement without written permission."
  ([exponential.earth press kit](https://exponential.earth/about/press-kit))

## Conflicts of interest

A conflict exists when your own gain could bend what you tell viewers. Handle it in four steps:

1. **Disclose** it.
2. **Separate** information from offers: show the product fully before you mention your services.
3. **Name alternatives**: the GPL lets anyone host, extend and support Exponential, and you can say so
   ([Who is 7x?, Open source licenses](../who-is-7x.md#open-source-licenses)).
4. **Step back** when the conflict is strong, for example a comparison where you sell one of the products.

## What the sources say about programs and payments

- No creator, influencer or affiliate program is documented. Do not tell viewers that one exists.
- The Exponential Partner Program, for companies, pays lead referral commissions of 10% of the total contract value
  for a cold lead and 20% for a hot lead ([Referrals and leads](../partners/referrals-and-leads.md);
  [Partner plans and prices](../partners/partner-plans-and-prices.md#partner-terms)). If you are a partner, that is a
  tie to disclose.
- The two organizations take sponsorship on GitHub ([Sponsors: start here](../sponsors/sponsors-start-here.md)).
  Sponsorship flows to them, not to creators.
