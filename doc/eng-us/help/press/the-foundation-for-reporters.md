## In one paragraph

The Exponential Foundation is a nonprofit corporation that describes itself as "a 501(c)(3) Non-Profit Corporation
founded in Seattle, Washington, USA". It was founded on 19 March 2026. It operates the Exponential open source
project and funds its development, documentation and community; 7x, the company that leads development, is its main
contributor. Sources: <https://foundation.exponential.earth/>, <https://exponential.earth/about/project>.

## What it funds

The se7enxweb profile lists what a sponsorship of the Foundation pays for: core library maintenance, API and core
documentation, GraphQL, REST and Solr integration layers, community infrastructure, and Zeta Components security
hardening ([What the Foundation funds](../who-is-the-exponential-foundation.md#what-the-foundation-funds)). Its sponsor
page is <https://github.com/sponsors/exponentialcms>. See [Writing about funding and sponsors](writing-about-funding-and-sponsors.md).

## How it relates to 7x

Two organizations, one project: 7x is a company, the Foundation a nonprofit. The Foundation names 7x "the current
leader in contributions both financial, code, extension development, new features, infrastructure, maintenance and
much much more back to the project". Both hold the copyright together ("Exponential Foundation and 7x"). Details:
[Two organizations, one project](two-organizations-one-project.md).

## Names and spelling

- Write **the Exponential Foundation**; after the first mention, **the Foundation**.
- Never "the 7x Foundation", "Exponential Inc." or "the Exponential company".
- Never write that the Foundation sells services; that is 7x.

## What the sources do not say

The public sources used for this help do not name the Foundation's board, describe its governance, list members
besides its supporters, or publish a budget ([What the sources do not say](../who-is-the-exponential-foundation.md#what-the-sources-do-not-say)).
Its website says it has "a long detailed list of growth goals for the Exponential Project in 2026" without listing
them. Do not fill these gaps; ask, and attribute the answer.

The Foundation's postal address and registration number are published on its websites; this help does not repeat
them, and a story rarely needs them.
