# Verifying quotes

This page is for reporters and fact-checkers. A quote must be real, exact, in context and correctly attributed. With
open source projects, many "quotes" are really sentences from a README, a forum post or an old press release; each of
those is quotable, but only as what it is.

## Four checks for every quote

1. **Real**: there is an original: a page, a file, a recording, an e-mail.
2. **Exact**: the words match the original, including its spelling. Omissions are marked with "...".
3. **In context**: the surrounding text does not change its meaning.
4. **Attributed**: the speaker or the document is named correctly, with role and date ([Attributing statements](attributing-statements.md)).

## Checking quotes from published pages

- Open the original page; do not copy from another article.
- For a repository file, cite the file and, if precision matters, the commit:
  `https://github.com/se7enxweb/exponential/blob/<commit>/README.md`.
- Websites change: save the date you read it, and an archive copy if your outlet uses one.

## Checking quotes from interviews

- Record with consent, or take verbatim notes.
- If you clean up spoken language, do not change the meaning; your outlet's rules decide how much cleaning is allowed.
- If you send quotes back for a factual check, say what you will accept as a correction (facts) and what not (tone).

## Quotes that circulate without a source

If a quote appears in a forum, a comment or another article without a source, treat it as unverified. Ask the person
or organization whether they said it ([Quotes policy](quotes-policy.md)). Do not use it until confirmed.
