# Building a portfolio and getting work

This page is for theme designers who want to work on Exponential projects: freelancers, designers in agencies, and
students. It covers what makes a strong portfolio piece for a content-managed site, how open source work shows your
skill, and the channels that exist today. It does not promise programs that are not published; the partner program
pages say what exists ([Partners: start here](../partners/partners-start-here.md)).

## What clients look for

Organizations that choose a content management system want a designer who understands that editors fill the site.
Show that you can:

- design for content at its extremes ([Designing with real content](designing-with-real-content.md));
- design systems, not pages: tokens, components, view modes ([Component inventory](component-inventory.md));
- deliver accessible, dark-mode-ready, multilingual work;
- work with developers and hand over cleanly ([Handoff checklist](handoff-checklist.md)).

## Portfolio pieces that show it

| Piece | What it proves |
|---|---|
| A case study of one site: problem, content model, design system, result | you design for content and editors |
| The component inventory and token sheet of a project | you design systems |
| Before and after with real editor content | your design survives real use |
| Light and dark, desktop and phone, left to right and right to left | breadth and care |
| A published theme on GitHub with a demo | you finish and maintain things |

A case study is stronger with facts: what changed, measured how. The partner pages explain how to write one others
can trust ([Writing a case study](../partners/writing-a-case-study.md)).

## Showing work without exposing clients

- Ask for written permission before you show client work; contracts often decide this ([Contracts and terms](../designers/sales/contracts-and-terms.md)).
- Never show real customer or personal data in screenshots: replace names, addresses and user data, and use
  `example.invalid` addresses in any demo accounts ([Customer data and privacy](../designers/sales/customer-data-and-privacy.md)).
- If you may not show the work, show the method: a rebuilt, anonymized version of the component inventory or a
  similar theme of your own.

## Open source as portfolio

Public work is the most verifiable portfolio:

- **Publish a theme.** A small, well-made theme with a demo site, documentation and a clear license shows the whole
  craft ([Distributing a theme: start here](../designers/distribution/distribution-start-here.md),
  [Publishing a theme](../partners/publishing-a-theme.md)).
- **Contribute back.** Fix an accessibility or dark-mode issue in a shipped design, improve the designer help, or
  translate it ([Contributing back upstream](../designers/lifecycle/contributing-back-upstream.md),
  [Free documentation](../free-documentation.md)).
- **Answer questions** in the community portal's design forum
  ([Announcing a theme on share.exponential.earth](../designers/distribution/announcing-on-share-exponential-earth.md)).

## Where Exponential work is found

There is no official theme marketplace. Themes and their authors are found through GitHub, Packagist, the community
portal, project listings, demo sites and authors' own sites; the designer sales section describes these channels as
they are ([Where Exponential themes are found today](../designers/sales/where-themes-are-found.md)). Much work comes
through agencies and implementation partners who need design capacity
([Working with agencies and partners](../designers/sales/working-with-agencies-and-partners.md)).

## The partner program

The Exponential Partner Program has a **creative partner** profile for design studios and agencies
([Creative partners](../partners/creative-partners.md)) and a profile for those who publish extensions and themes
([Extension and theme partners](../partners/extension-and-theme-partners.md)). The program names a Design
certification, but its syllabus and exams are not published; do not present any course or self-made certificate as
that certification ([Certifications](../partners/certifications.md)). Students have their own level
([Student and education partners](../partners/student-and-education-partners.md)). Levels, benefits and prices are on
the partner pages; this page does not repeat them.

## A ninety-day plan

1. **Days 1 to 30:** work through the [reading paths](reading-paths-for-theme-designers.md); build a small theme on a
   development installation, with tokens, dark mode and a style guide page.
2. **Days 31 to 60:** publish it with a README, license and screenshots; fix one issue in a shipped design or the
   help and submit it.
3. **Days 61 to 90:** write one case study (your own theme counts), announce the theme on the community portal, and
   contact two agencies or partners with the case study.
